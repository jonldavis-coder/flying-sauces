New England Shore

Drawn butter, cranberry, and a later clam sauce.

English Puritan settlers in the seventeenth century met a cold coast: dairy, cod, lobster, and native cranberry bogs already used by Wampanoag and other Native people. Drawn butter and cranberry sauce are the shore sauces in this guide.

White clam sauce is later Italian-American cooking on the same water. It is included so the book does not pretend the shore stopped in 1700. The sweet boiled cranberry sauce of Thanksgiving is a domestic American habit, not a menu from 1621.

Drawn Butter

Clarified butter: clean, rich, and lightly salty.

2 min prep · 8 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1/2 cup

History

Drawn butter is the melted, clarified butter served with boiled lobster and other New England seafood. In American kitchen language, drawn butter means the clear butterfat after the milk solids are skimmed. Coastal cooks served it because lobster and clams need a hot fat to carry salt, and butter was the local dairy fat.

Cultural background

A lobster dinner on the New England shore is the setting. Lemon is common and not required. The sauce is plain on purpose. The shellfish is the flavor.

Why it tastes this way

Clarifying removes the milk solids, so the butter tastes clean and does not scorch. Salt and, if you add it, lemon are the only seasonings. That bareness is why it works on sweet lobster.

How it is made

Butter is melted, the foam is skimmed, and the clear fat is poured off.

Authentic touch. Melt the butter slowly and skim the foam. Leave the cloudy solids in the bottom of the pan and pour off the clear fat. Serve it warm, not browned.

How it is used

Serve it warm with boiled lobster or steamed clams.

Field notes

  • Drawn butter is clarified butter, which is why it stays clear.
  • It is a shore sauce for shellfish, not a seasoned gravy.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup unsalted butter
  • Pinch of salt
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice, optional

Steps

  1. Melt the butter over low heat.
  2. Skim the foam.
  3. Pour the clear fat into a warm cup, leaving the solids behind.
  4. Add salt and lemon if you want them.
  5. Serve warm.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • Some tables serve simply melted butter and still call it drawn.
  • Browned butter is a different sauce.

Pairings and uses

  • Seafood
  • Dipping
  • boiled lobster
  • steamed clams
  • lemon
  • Vegetarian
  • Gluten-free
  • Nut-free

Cranberry Sauce

Cranberry and sugar: tart, bright, and jammy.

5 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 8 · about 2 cups

History

Cranberry sauce is the tart fruit sauce of the American Thanksgiving table, served with roast turkey. Cranberries grow in northern bogs. Sugar balances their sharpness. A jellied slice and a whole-berry sauce are both traditional. This is the whole-berry simmer.

Cultural background

Turkey is rich and mild. The sauce is sour enough to cut it. Orange peel is a common perfume and not required.

Why it tastes this way

Cranberries are intensely tart. Sugar and a short simmer pop the berries and make a loose jam. Too much sugar turns it into candy and it stops cutting the turkey.

How it is made

Cranberries, sugar, and water simmer until the berries burst.

Authentic touch. Simmer until most berries burst and the sauce thickens. Keep it tart. Chill it before dinner.

How it is used

Serve it cold or cool with roast turkey.

Field notes

  • The berries should still taste tart.
  • Whole-berry sauce and the sliced jelly are both old American forms.

Ingredients

  • 12 ounces cranberries
  • 3/4 cup sugar
  • 3/4 cup water
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Simmer the cranberries, sugar, water, and salt.
  2. Cook until most berries burst, about 10 minutes.
  3. Cool. It will thicken.
  4. Serve with turkey.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • Orange zest is a common addition.
  • A jellied loaf is the molded version.

Pairings and uses

  • Meat
  • Roasts
  • roast turkey
  • stuffing
  • Vegetarian
  • Vegan
  • Gluten-free
  • Dairy-free
  • Nut-free

White Clam Sauce

Clam, garlic, white wine, and olive oil: briny and sharp.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 pasta portions

History

White clam sauce is the Italian-American pasta sauce of clams, garlic, olive oil, and often white wine, associated with coastal cities and with linguine. Red clam sauce adds tomato. The white version tastes of the clam liquor. It is a fast sauce.

Cultural background

Linguine alle vongole in Italy is the related dish. The American white sauce often uses canned clam juice when fresh littlenecks are out of season. The juice is the flavor.

Why it tastes this way

Clam liquor is salty and oceanic. Garlic and chile in olive oil perfume it. Wine sharpens it. A cream sauce would hide the brine, which is why the classic white sauce has little or no cream.

How it is made

Garlic and chile warm in oil, clam juice and wine reduce, and clams and parsley finish the pan.

Authentic touch. Add the clam juice and let it reduce a little. Put the clam meats in at the end so they stay tender. Toss with pasta water.

How it is used

Toss it with linguine.

Field notes

  • The juice is the sauce. The clam meats are the garnish.
  • Add the meats late so they do not turn rubbery.

Ingredients

  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1/2 teaspoon chile flakes
  • 1/2 cup dry white wine
  • 1 cup clam juice
  • 2 cans (6 ounces) chopped clams, drained, juice reserved
  • 2 tablespoons parsley
  • 12 ounces linguine

Steps

  1. Warm the oil, garlic, and chile.
  2. Add the wine and clam juice, including juice from the cans, and simmer 5 minutes.
  3. Boil the linguine.
  4. Stir in the clam meats and parsley.
  5. Toss with the pasta and a splash of pasta water.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. Home cooking, not a professional food-safety instruction.

Variations

  • Red clam sauce adds tomato.
  • A splash of cream is a later restaurant habit.

Pairings and uses

  • Pasta
  • Seafood
  • linguine
  • parsley
  • lemon
  • Dairy-free
  • Nut-free
