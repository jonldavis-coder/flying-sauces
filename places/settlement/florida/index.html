Who settled the flavor

Florida

Spanish St. Augustine, a Minorcan tradition told carefully, and Cuban kitchens.

Spain founded St. Augustine in 1565, the oldest continuously occupied European settlement in what is now the United States. Florida’s later map includes British periods, American statehood, and a long Cuban presence. Cuban communities grew through the nineteenth century and, in great numbers, after 1959. Their mojo criollo is a Cuban sauce cooked in Florida as well as on the island. The page keeps the Cuban name. It does not rebrand mojo as a Floridian invention.

Datil pepper sauce belongs to St. Augustine. The datil is a very hot, fruity chile, and local tradition says Minorcan settlers brought it. That story is traditional. It is not a proven botanical history, so this guide does not state it as fact. The sauce itself is real: datil, vinegar, and garlic, served with fried fish.

A tomato-ketchup hot sauce would hide the fruit. The datil page says so. Cuban sofrito and the enchilado sauce are Cuban recipes in the Caribbean chapter, linked here because Cuban Florida cooks them, not because they were born in St. Augustine.
