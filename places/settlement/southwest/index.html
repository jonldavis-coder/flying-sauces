Who settled the flavor

Southwest

Chile before the Spanish border, then New Mexican and Mexican sauces labeled honestly.

Chile was grown in the Americas, including the Pueblo and other Native farming regions of the Southwest, long before Spanish colonization. Spanish settlement and, later, Mexican kitchens changed how those chiles were cooked into sauces. New Mexico’s red chile sauce and green chile sauce are the Southwestern sauces in this guide: dried pods on one page, flame-roasted fresh chiles on the other. Cumin is easy to overdo in the red sauce. The chile is the body.

Mexican moles, salsas, and pipián are Mexican. They are the related sauces across a border that is newer than the chile. They are labeled Mexican on their own pages. This chapter links them so a reader can see the kinship. It does not move mole poblano into New Mexico.

Texas mop sauce is a Central Texas barbecue baste, thinner and less sweet than Kansas City sauce. It is a neighbor on the southern plains, not a New Mexican chile sauce, and it lives on the Southern page as well as in the barbecue book.
