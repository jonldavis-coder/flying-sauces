Who settled the flavor

New England

English settlement, a cold coast, dairy, and cranberries.

English Puritans and other English settlers colonized the New England coast in the seventeenth century. The climate was cold, the dairy herd mattered, and the shore supplied cod, clams, and lobster. Sauces stayed simple because the ingredients that keep were milk, butter, vinegar, and what the bogs grew.

The cranberry is a native bog fruit. English-speaking tables adopted it. Cranberry sauce, as a relish beside a roast, is the New England sauce in this guide. It is not a claim that a Puritan household wrote the modern Thanksgiving recipe in a known year. Drawn butter is the other shore sauce: clarified butter, often with lemon, for lobster and shellfish. The milk solids are removed so the butter can be warmed without burning.

Later immigrants changed the coast again. White clam sauce is Italian-American, a garlic and oil or cream sauce for pasta, not a Puritan dish. It belongs in the eastern story as a second settlement, and it is labeled that way on its own page.
