Chili Crab Sauce

Tomato, chile, ginger, and a little sweetness: savory, hot, and glossy.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 3 cups

History

Singapore chili crab is a tomato and chile sauce for crab, associated with the city’s seafood restaurants from the 1950s onward. Several cooks have been credited, and the stories do not line up cleanly, so this page does not name a single inventor. The sauce is tomato, chile, ginger, and garlic, often finished with egg.

Cultural background

The crab is cooked in the sauce, and diners use bread or mantou to wipe the plate. It is a special-occasion restaurant dish that home cooks also make.

Why it tastes this way

Tomato, ginger, garlic, and chile taste sweet, hot, and savory. Egg stirred in at the end makes it silky. The crab is mild, so the sauce is the dish. Origin stories about the first cook disagree, so none is asserted here.

How it is made

Aromatic ginger, garlic, and chile fry, tomato and sauces simmer, and a beaten egg can be stirred in at the end.

Authentic touch

Fry the ginger and garlic, simmer the tomato, and stir the egg off a hard boil so it streaks instead of scrambling.

How it is used

Toss cracked cooked crab in the sauce, or simmer the crab in it.

Field notes

  • Chili crab is a Singapore restaurant dish of the mid-twentieth century.
  • The sauce is tomato-chile, not a dried-chile sambal alone.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1 tablespoon grated ginger
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 2 red chiles, minced
  • 1 cup tomato puree
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 1 egg, beaten
  • Cooked crab, to serve

Steps

  1. Fry the ginger, garlic, and chile in the oil.
  2. Add the tomato, soy, sugar, and water and simmer 8 minutes.
  3. Stir in the egg off the boil so it streaks.
  4. Toss with hot cooked crab.
  5. Serve with bread for the sauce.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Black pepper crab is a different Singapore sauce.
  • Egg stirred in at the end is the silky restaurant finish.

Pairings

  • crab
  • mantou
  • rice
