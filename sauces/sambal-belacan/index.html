Sambal Belacan

Chile and toasted belacan, with lime: hot, pungent, and sharp.

15 min prep · 5 min cook · Serves 8 · about 3/4 cup

History

Sambal belacan is the Malaysian and Singaporean chile paste toasted with belacan, fermented shrimp paste, and finished with lime or calamansi. It is on the table for nasi lemak and for chili crab’s wider family of seafood meals. Toasting the belacan is essential.

Cultural background

A dab goes with rice, cucumber, and fried fish. It is sharper and fresher than a long-cooked sambal if the chiles stay partly raw after the paste is toasted.

Why it tastes this way

Toasted belacan smells nutty instead of only raw, and lime makes the chile sharp. It is a table paste for rice, used by the spoonful.

How it is made

Belacan is toasted, pounded with chile and salt, and mixed with lime.

Authentic touch

Toast the belacan until fragrant before you pound it with the chiles. Add lime at the end.

How it is used

Serve it with nasi lemak or fried fish.

Field notes

  • Belacan is toasted so it smells nutty instead of only raw.
  • It is a table sambal for nasi lemak, used by the spoonful.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon belacan
  • 6 red chiles
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon lime juice

Steps

  1. Toast the belacan in a dry pan until fragrant.
  2. Pound it with the chiles and salt.
  3. Stir in the lime.
  4. Taste.
  5. Serve with rice.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Calamansi instead of lime is the usual acid when you can get it.
  • A fried sambal belacan is darker and sweeter.

Pairings

  • nasi lemak
  • cucumber
  • fried fish
