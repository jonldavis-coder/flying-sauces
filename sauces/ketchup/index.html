Tomato Ketchup

Cooked tomato, vinegar, and warm spice: sweet, sour, and smooth.

History

English ketchups of the eighteenth century were often mushroom, walnut, or fish sauces. The name is linked to words for fish sauce in Southeast Asia and southern China, borrowed into English. Tomato ketchup became the American table sauce in the nineteenth century. Henry J. Heinz began selling his tomato ketchup in 1876, and the sweet cooked style became the default.

Cultural background

It is the sauce of American burgers, fries, and meatloaf. This recipe is a home cooked ketchup, not a copy of a factory formula.

Why it tastes this way

Long cooking with vinegar and sugar turns tomato from sharp and watery into sweet, sour, and smooth. Earlier English ketchups were mushroom or fish. The American taste is the tomato version of that idea.

How it is made

Tomatoes, vinegar, sugar, and spices simmer until thick, then the sauce is blended and simmered again.

Authentic touch

Simmer until thick. A short boil tastes like seasoned tomato juice, not ketchup.

How it is used

Use it on burgers and fries, or as the base of cocktail sauce and barbecue sauce.

Field notes

  • Early English ketchup was not necessarily made of tomato.
  • The sweet tomato style became the American standard in the nineteenth century.

Ingredients

  • 1 can (28 ounces) tomato puree
  • 1/3 cup apple cider vinegar
  • 3 tablespoons brown sugar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon mustard powder
  • Pinch of clove
  • Pinch of cinnamon

Steps

  1. Stir all the ingredients in a saucepan.
  2. Simmer 40 minutes, stirring, until thick.
  3. Blend until smooth if you want a bottled texture.
  4. Simmer 5 minutes more.
  5. Cool and refrigerate.

Refrigerate and use within three weeks. This is not a canning recipe. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Mushroom ketchup is the older English cousin.
  • Cocktail sauce adds horseradish and is a separate sauce.

Pairings

  • burgers
  • fries
  • meatloaf
  • fried potatoes
