Salsa Criolla

Onion, tomato, and vinegar: sharp, fresh, and juicy.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 2 cups

History

Argentine salsa criolla is a chopped onion and tomato relish with vinegar or oil, served with asado beside chimichurri. Peruvian salsa criolla is a related onion relish, often without tomato. This page is the Argentine tomato-onion version.

Cultural background

The onion is the main bite next to grilled beef. A soak in cold water or vinegar softens its harshness. It is raw.

Why it tastes this way

Raw onion tastes sharp and sweet once it is rinsed or dressed with vinegar. Tomato adds juice. Oil is optional. It stays fresh because it is not cooked.

How it is made

Onion is rinsed or pickled briefly, then mixed with tomato, vinegar, and salt.

Authentic touch

Slice the onion very thin, rinse it, and dress it with vinegar and salt before the tomato goes in.

How it is used

Serve it with grilled meat.

Field notes

  • It is the onion relish next to chimichurri, not a replacement.
  • Rinsing the onion is the usual kindness.

Ingredients

  • 1 red onion, thinly sliced and rinsed
  • 2 tomatoes, diced
  • 2 tablespoons vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons chopped parsley

Steps

  1. Rinse the onion and drain it.
  2. Toss with tomato, vinegar, oil, salt, and parsley.
  3. Rest 15 minutes.
  4. Serve with grilled meat.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Peruvian criolla is often onion, lime, and chile.
  • A bell pepper can join the Argentine bowl.

Pairings

  • asado
  • chimichurri
  • bread
