Cantonese Sweet and Sour Sauce

Vinegar, sugar, and fruit juice: sharp, sweet, and glossy.

5 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Cantonese sweet and sour sauce is vinegar and sugar, often with pineapple or hawthorne and ketchup in newer pots, glossed with starch. It sauces pork, fish, and spareribs. The bright red American Chinese version is a descendant and is usually thicker and sweeter. This is a lighter vinegar-forward sauce.

Cultural background

The meat is often fried and then coated, or the sauce is poured on at the table. Balance matters: if it is only sweet, it is not the Cantonese sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Vinegar and sugar are the taste. Fruit juice rounds them. If the sauce is only sweet, it is not Cantonese sweet and sour. Ketchup reddens some versions. It is not required for the balance.

How it is made

Vinegar, sugar, and pineapple juice simmer, then a cornstarch slurry makes the sauce glossy.

Authentic touch

Taste and add vinegar until the sour stands up to the sugar. Thicken only at the end.

How it is used

Toss it with fried pork or fish just before serving.

Field notes

  • A proper sweet and sour sauce is still sour.
  • The red color of many takeout sauces comes from ketchup, not from the vinegar.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup pineapple juice
  • 1/4 cup rice vinegar
  • 3 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 tablespoon ketchup
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch mixed with 1 tablespoon water
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Simmer the juice, vinegar, sugar, ketchup, water, and salt.
  2. Stir in the slurry and cook until glossy.
  3. Taste. Add vinegar if it is only sweet.
  4. Toss with just-fried pork or fish.
  5. Serve at once.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Ketchup makes the red restaurant color.
  • Hawthorne ketchup is a traditional Cantonese sour note when you can find it.

Pairings

  • fried pork
  • fried fish
  • pineapple
