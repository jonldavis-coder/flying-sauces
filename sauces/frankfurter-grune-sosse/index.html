Frankfurter Grüne Soße

Seven herbs, sour cream or yogurt, and egg: fresh, tangy, and green.

History

Frankfurter Grüne Soße is the cold herb sauce of Frankfurt, traditionally made with seven herbs: borage, chervil, cress, parsley, salad burnet, sorrel, and chives, though the exact seven vary by cook and season. It is eaten with potatoes and peeled hard-boiled eggs, and with beef. A local story linking it to Goethe’s mother is local lore and is not treated here as fact.

Cultural background

It is a spring and summer dish of Hesse. The herbs are chopped very fine. Sour cream, yogurt, or a mixture binds them, often with a chopped egg.

Why it tastes this way

A large amount of chopped herbs in sour cream or yogurt tastes green and tangy. Seven herbs are traditional when the market has them. The egg adds body. It is cold, so the herbs stay fresh.

How it is made

A large handful of mixed herbs is chopped and stirred into sour cream or yogurt with vinegar, mustard, and chopped egg.

Authentic touch

Chop the herbs very fine and use a lot of them. A few leaves stirred into yogurt will not taste like green sauce.

How it is used

Serve it cold with boiled potatoes and hard-boiled eggs, or with cold beef.

Field notes

  • The traditional count is seven herbs, and the list shifts with the market.
  • It is a cold sauce for potatoes and eggs, not a cooked green gravy.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups mixed herbs, finely chopped (parsley, chives, chervil, sorrel, tarragon)
  • 1/2 cup sour cream
  • 1/2 cup plain yogurt
  • 1 teaspoon mustard
  • 1 tablespoon white vinegar
  • 1 hard-boiled egg, chopped
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Chop the herbs very fine.
  2. Stir them with the sour cream, yogurt, mustard, and vinegar.
  3. Fold in the egg.
  4. Salt to taste and rest 15 minutes.
  5. Serve cold with potatoes.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • If borage and burnet are missing, parsley, chives, sorrel, chervil, and tarragon still make a Frankfurt-style sauce.
  • Buttermilk makes it looser.

Pairings

  • boiled potatoes
  • hard-boiled eggs
  • cold roast beef
