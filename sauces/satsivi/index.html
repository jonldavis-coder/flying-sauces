Satsivi

Walnut, garlic, vinegar, and warm spice: thick, tangy, and aromatic.

25 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · about 3 cups

History

Satsivi is the cold walnut sauce of Georgia, served with poultry, especially at large meals and at New Year. Walnuts are pounded with garlic, vinegar, and spices that often include coriander, cinnamon, and marigold (Imeretian saffron). The name refers to food served cold.

Cultural background

A turkey or chicken is boiled or roasted, and the cooking liquid loosens the walnut paste. The dish cools before it is served. Bazhe is a simpler walnut sauce and is listed as a cousin, not as the same recipe.

Why it tastes this way

Walnuts, pounded with garlic, vinegar, and warm spices, thicken the sauce without cream. It is served cool, which sets the walnut paste and softens the garlic. The spices are the Georgian signature.

How it is made

Walnuts and garlic are ground fine, loosened with broth, seasoned, and simmered briefly, then cooled.

Authentic touch

Grind the walnuts fine and simmer them long enough to lose the raw nut taste. Then cool the sauce fully before you serve it.

How it is used

Pour it over boiled or roasted chicken or turkey and serve it cool or at room temperature.

Field notes

  • Satsivi is meant to be eaten cool, which sets the walnut sauce.
  • Walnuts, not cream, make it thick.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups walnuts
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1/4 teaspoon cayenne
  • 2 tablespoons white wine vinegar
  • 2 cups chicken broth, cooled
  • Salt
  • 1 egg yolk

Steps

  1. Grind the walnuts and garlic to a paste.
  2. Stir in the spices, vinegar, and enough broth to make a thick sauce.
  3. Simmer 10 minutes, stirring.
  4. Take it off the heat, temper the yolk with a little sauce, and stir it back in. Do not boil.
  5. Salt, cool completely, and serve over poultry.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Bazhe is a quicker walnut sauce for vegetables and fish.
  • Ground marigold is traditional. A pinch of saffron is only a color cousin, not the same spice.

Pairings

  • boiled chicken
  • turkey
  • gomi or bread
