Dan Dan Sauce

The next field stop is a nearby sauce on the same map.

Chile oil, sesame paste, and preserved vegetable: hot, nutty, and savory.

15 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · 4 noodle bowls

  • Dairy-free

History

Dan dan noodles are a Sichuan street dish. The sauce in the bowl is chile oil, Sichuan pepper, sesame paste, soy, and often ya cai, preserved mustard greens, with a spoon of seasoned ground pork on top. The name refers to the pole vendors once used to carry the noodles. The sauce is mixed by the eater.

Cultural background

Each bowl gets sauce at the bottom, noodles on top, and pork. You stir before eating. It should be hot and numbing, not creamy like a peanut satay sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Chile oil and Sichuan pepper taste hot and numbing. Sesame paste tastes nutty. Preserved vegetable and pork taste savory. You mix the bowl, so the sauce stays concentrated at the bottom until you stir.

How it is made

Sesame paste is loosened with soy and chile oil, and seasoned pork is spooned on the noodles.

Authentic touch

Put the sauce in the bowl first, then the noodles. Stir before you eat. Do not drown it in broth.

How it is used

Put sauce in a bowl, add hot noodles and the pork, and stir.

Field notes

  • Dan dan refers to the carrying pole of street vendors.
  • You mix the bowl yourself. The sauce is not tossed in a pan like lo mein.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons sesame paste
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon Chinkiang vinegar
  • 2 tablespoons chile oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground Sichuan pepper
  • 4 ounces ground pork
  • 1 tablespoon minced pickled mustard greens
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced

Steps

  1. Stir the sesame paste with the soy, vinegar, chile oil, and Sichuan pepper.
  2. Fry the pork with the garlic and pickled greens until cooked.
  3. Boil noodles.
  4. Put sauce and pork in each bowl and top with noodles.
  5. Stir before eating.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Ya cai is the traditional preserved vegetable. A little pickled mustard is the stand-in.
  • A pork-free bowl is a different, less classic dish.

Pairings

  • wheat noodles
  • pickled mustard greens
  • scallions
