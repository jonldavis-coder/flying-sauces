Irish Whiskey Sauce

Whiskey, cream, and shallot: sharp, rich, and a little sweet.

5 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1 cup

History

Irish whiskey cream sauce is a steak sauce of restaurants and home kitchens in Ireland. Cream, a splash of Irish whiskey, and stock or pan juices are the usual parts. It is not a medieval farm recipe, and this guide does not name an inventor. The whiskey is the Irish part. The cream is the body.

Cultural background

It is cooked in the same pan as a steak so the browned bits dissolve into the cream. The alcohol has to cook off. What remains is grain and oak, not a spoon of raw spirit.

Why it tastes this way

Whiskey brings a grain sweetness and a sharp edge. Cream softens that edge and makes a sauce that clings. Shallot keeps it savory. If the whiskey is not simmered, the sauce tastes of raw alcohol instead of the grain. Stock, or the steak juices, is why it tastes of meat and not of dessert cream.

How it is made

Shallot softens in butter, whiskey reduces, then stock and cream simmer into a spoonable sauce.

Authentic touch

Simmer until the raw alcohol smell is gone and the cream thickens enough to coat a spoon. Use a whiskey you would drink. A flavored liqueur makes a different sauce.

How it is used

Spoon it over pan-seared steak. Do not pour it on while the whiskey is still raw.

Field notes

  • This is a restaurant and home sauce, not an ancient recipe with a known author.
  • The cream is cooked with the whiskey. It is not whipped cream on a pudding.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 1 shallot, minced
  • 1/4 cup Irish whiskey
  • 1/2 cup beef stock
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
  • Black pepper
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Soften the shallot in the butter.
  2. Add the whiskey and simmer 1 minute.
  3. Add the stock and reduce by half.
  4. Stir in the cream and simmer until the sauce coats a spoon.
  5. Season with pepper and a little salt.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A teaspoon of whole-grain mustard is a common addition.
  • Mushrooms can cook with the shallot.

Pairings

  • steak
  • mushrooms
  • potatoes
