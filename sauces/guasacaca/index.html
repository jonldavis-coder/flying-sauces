Guasacaca

Avocado, vinegar, and bell pepper: bright, herby, and loose.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 2 cups

History

Guasacaca is the Venezuelan avocado sauce for grilled meat. Vinegar or lime, bell pepper, onion, and cilantro lighten the avocado. It is usually looser and sharper than Mexican guacamole.

Cultural background

It is spooned over parrilla meats. The vinegar is why it tastes more like a sauce than a thick dip. Some versions are mostly herb and pepper with only a little avocado.

Why it tastes this way

Avocado fat is cut with vinegar, so the sauce tastes sharp instead of only rich. Bell pepper and cilantro keep it fresh. A thick guacamole has less acid and more fruit.

How it is made

Avocado, pepper, onion, cilantro, and vinegar are chopped or briefly blended.

Authentic touch

Blend or chop it looser than guacamole and do not skip the vinegar. Salt it enough for grilled meat.

How it is used

Serve it with grilled chicken or beef.

Field notes

  • It is an avocado sauce and not the same balance as guacamole.
  • Vinegar is the sharp edge.

Ingredients

  • 2 ripe avocados
  • 1/2 green bell pepper
  • 1/4 onion
  • 1/2 cup cilantro
  • 2 tablespoons vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon oil

Steps

  1. Chop or pulse everything to a coarse sauce.
  2. It should be loose enough to spoon.
  3. Taste for vinegar and salt.
  4. Serve the same day.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A fully chunky version is more like a relish.
  • Without avocado it becomes a different green sauce.

Pairings

  • parrilla
  • arepas
  • yuca
