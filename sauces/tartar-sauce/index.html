Tartar Sauce

Mayonnaise, pickle, and capers: creamy, sharp, and briny.

10 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 1 cup

History

Tartar sauce is mayonnaise with chopped pickle, capers, and sometimes shallot, served with fried fish. The name is older than the American fish fry, and British and French tables have related sauces. The pickles are why it tastes sharper than plain mayonnaise.

Cultural background

It is the sauce next to fried fish and fish sticks. The chop should be fine. A sweet relish makes it closer to Thousand Island.

Why it tastes this way

Mayonnaise is rich. Sour pickle and capers add acid and brine that cut fried batter. Lemon brightens it. Without the pickle, it is just mayonnaise.

How it is made

Mayonnaise is mixed with pickle, capers, and lemon.

Authentic touch

Chop the pickle and capers very fine and fold them in. Use a sour pickle, not a sweet one.

How it is used

Serve it with fried fish.

Field notes

  • The pickles are the point.
  • A sweet relish changes it into a different pink sauce.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup mayonnaise
  • 2 tablespoons finely chopped sour pickle
  • 1 tablespoon capers, chopped
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice
  • 1 tablespoon chopped parsley
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Stir everything together.
  2. Rest 15 minutes.
  3. Taste.
  4. Serve cold with fish.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Dill instead of parsley is a common herb.
  • Yogurt lightens it and adds a dairy note.

Pairings

  • fried fish
  • fish sandwiches
  • potato cakes
