Haitian Epis

Bell pepper, garlic, thyme, and scallion: savory and herbal.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 10 · about 2 cups

History

Epis is the Haitian seasoning base of pepper, garlic, scallion, thyme, and often parsley or culantro, blended and used to start stews and marinades. It plays a role like a sofrito. Cooks keep it on hand. The blend is not one fixed recipe.

Cultural background

Rice, beans, chicken, and griot often start with epis fried in oil. That fry is when the raw blend becomes the flavor of the pot.

Why it tastes this way

A concentrated herb and allium paste tastes savory once it fries in oil. Bell pepper sweetens it. Thyme is the herbal note. Using it raw as a table sauce is not the usual path.

How it is made

Pepper, garlic, herbs, and scallion are blended, then fried as the base of a dish.

Authentic touch

Blend it, then fry a spoonful in oil at the start of a stew until it smells sweet. That fried spoonful seasons the whole pot.

How it is used

Use it to start rice and stews or as a marinade that you then cook.

Field notes

  • Epis is a cooking base, like a sofrito.
  • Frying it is what turns the raw blend into the stew’s flavor.

Ingredients

  • 1 green bell pepper
  • 1 onion
  • 6 scallions
  • 6 garlic cloves
  • 1 cup parsley
  • 6 sprigs thyme
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Blend everything except a spare spoon of oil, adding a splash of water if needed.
  2. Store it cold.
  3. When you cook, fry a few tablespoons in oil until fragrant.
  4. Build the stew on that base.
  5. Use the jar within a few days.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Trinidadian green seasoning is a close cousin.
  • A Scotch bonnet in the blend makes it hot.

Pairings

  • diri ak pwa
  • chicken
  • pork griot
