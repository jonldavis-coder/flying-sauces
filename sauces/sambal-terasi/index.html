Sambal Terasi

The next field stop is a nearby sauce on the same map.

Chile, tomato, and shrimp paste: hot, pungent, and savory.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 8 · about 1 cup

History

Sambal terasi is the Indonesian chile sauce cooked with terasi, fermented shrimp paste. It is fried so the paste loses its raw edge. Sambal oelek is the plain crushed chile without shrimp paste. Terasi is what makes this one smell like a Javanese table.

Cultural background

It is eaten with rice, lalapan vegetables, and fried fish. A little is enough. The shrimp paste must be cooked.

Why it tastes this way

Toasted shrimp paste tastes savory and pungent. Fried chile and tomato make it hot and a little sweet. Cooking the paste is why it does not taste raw. Sambal oelek has no shrimp paste.

How it is made

Terasi is toasted, then fried with chile, shallot, garlic, and tomato until the sauce is dark and jammy.

Authentic touch

Toast the terasi, then fry the blended chile until the raw smell is gone.

How it is used

Serve it at room temperature with rice and fried fish.

Field notes

  • Terasi has to be cooked. Raw shrimp paste in this sauce is not the usual result.
  • Sambal oelek and sambal terasi are different sauces.

Ingredients

  • 1 teaspoon terasi (shrimp paste)
  • 6 red chiles
  • 2 shallots
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 tomato
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon lime juice

Steps

  1. Wrap the terasi in foil and toast it in a dry pan 2 minutes.
  2. Blend it with the chiles, shallots, garlic, and tomato.
  3. Fry the paste in the oil until darker and fragrant, about 8 minutes.
  4. Add salt and lime.
  5. Cool and serve with rice.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Sambal oelek has no shrimp paste.
  • Lime at the end brightens a very rich batch.

Pairings

  • rice
  • fried fish
  • raw cabbage and cucumber
