Hot Bacon Dressing

Bacon fat, vinegar, and sugar: smoky, sharp, and warm.

5 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1 cup

History

Hot bacon dressing is the Pennsylvania Dutch dressing for wilted lettuce, dandelion, or spinach. Bacon fat, vinegar, and sugar are warmed and poured over the greens so they soften. Pennsylvania Dutch means the German-speaking settlers of Pennsylvania, from Deutsch, not the Netherlands. The dressing is a home sauce of that community.

Cultural background

Spring dandelion and garden lettuce were the greens. The warm fat wilts them. Vinegar and sugar are the sweet-sour German-American balance.

Why it tastes this way

Bacon fat tastes smoky and salty. Vinegar sharpens it and sugar rounds it. Heat is what wilts the greens, so the dressing has to be poured on while it is hot. A cold mayonnaise dressing will not do this job.

How it is made

Bacon is cooked, and vinegar and sugar are simmered in the fat.

Authentic touch

Pour the dressing over the greens at the table while it still steams, and toss at once.

How it is used

Pour it over lettuce, spinach, or dandelion greens.

Field notes

  • Pennsylvania Dutch is a German-American community. Dutch here comes from Deutsch.
  • The dressing is served hot so the greens wilt.

Ingredients

  • 4 slices bacon
  • 1 small onion, minced
  • 1/4 cup cider vinegar
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1/4 cup water
  • 1/4 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Cook the bacon until crisp. Remove it and leave 2 tablespoons of fat.
  2. Cook the onion in the fat until soft.
  3. Add the vinegar, sugar, water, salt, and pepper and simmer 2 minutes.
  4. Crumble the bacon back in.
  5. Pour at once over greens.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of flour makes a thicker dressing.
  • Onion cooked in the fat is common.

Pairings

  • leaf lettuce
  • dandelion greens
  • hard-boiled eggs
