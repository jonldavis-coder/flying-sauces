Ta'leya

Fried garlic and ghee with cumin: nutty, savory, and aromatic.

5 min prep · 5 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1/2 cup

History

Ta'leya is the Egyptian fried-garlic finish spooned over molokhia and sometimes over koshari. Garlic is fried in ghee or oil with cumin and coriander until golden. It is a finishing sauce, not a simmered gravy. The garlic has to stay golden. Dark garlic turns bitter.

Cultural background

Molokhia, the green soup, is the classic partner. The ta'leya hits the surface and perfumes the bowl. Many cooks use ghee. Oil works and changes the dairy status.

Why it tastes this way

Garlic fried in ghee until pale gold tastes sweet and nutty, and cumin blooms in the fat. That sizzle is poured over mild greens. Dark garlic tastes bitter.

How it is made

Garlic and spices fry quickly in ghee until the garlic is golden, then the whole sizzling mixture is poured over the dish.

Authentic touch

Stop at pale gold and pour it on immediately. The garlic keeps cooking in the hot fat.

How it is used

Pour it over molokhia or another green stew at the table.

Field notes

  • Ta'leya is a finish, poured on at the end so the garlic stays fragrant.
  • Golden garlic is the line. Brown garlic ruins it.

Ingredients

  • 3 tablespoons ghee
  • 6 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon ground coriander
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cumin
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Warm the ghee.
  2. Add the garlic and cook until pale gold.
  3. Add the coriander, cumin, and salt and cook a few seconds.
  4. Pour it at once over molokhia or the dish you are finishing.
  5. Do not hold it. The garlic keeps cooking in the hot fat.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Oil instead of ghee makes it dairy-free.
  • Coriander with the cumin is the usual spice pair.

Pairings

  • molokhia
  • koshari
  • rice
