Panang Curry

The next field stop is a nearby sauce on the same map.

Next stop: Thai Red Curry · Thailand

Red curry paste, peanut, and a little coconut: rich, salty, and mildly sweet.

15 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 4 · about 3 cups

  • Curry sauce
  • Medium
  • Dairy-free

History

Panang, or phanaeng, is a thicker Thai curry than a soupy red curry, often with peanut and a reduced coconut sauce. The name is associated with Penang in food histories, and the dish as cooked in Thailand is its own curry. It is drier and sweeter than a bowl of red curry.

Cultural background

It is eaten with rice. Kaffir lime leaf, when you have it, is the citrus perfume. The sauce should cling to the meat.

Why it tastes this way

Coconut milk reduced with curry paste tastes rich. Peanut adds body. Fish sauce and a little sugar make it salty-sweet. Less liquid is why it is thicker than red curry.

How it is made

Red curry paste fries in coconut cream, then meat, peanut, fish sauce, and sugar simmer until thick.

Authentic touch

Fry the paste in coconut cream and let the sauce reduce until it coats the meat. Tear lime leaf in at the end if you have it.

How it is used

Serve it with rice.

Field notes

  • Panang is thicker than a standard red curry.
  • Lime leaf is the fresh finish when you can get it.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 2 tablespoons red curry paste
  • 1 can (14 ounces) coconut milk
  • 1 pound chicken thighs
  • 2 tablespoons ground roasted peanuts
  • 1 tablespoon fish sauce
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 4 lime leaves, optional

Steps

  1. Fry the curry paste in the oil.
  2. Add a little coconut milk and fry until fragrant.
  3. Add the chicken and the rest of the coconut milk, peanuts, fish sauce, and sugar.
  4. Simmer until the chicken is cooked and the sauce is thick.
  5. Stir in torn lime leaf if using and serve with rice.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Red curry is soupier and usually has no peanut.
  • A spoon of ground peanut is the thickener here.

Pairings

  • jasmine rice
  • beef or chicken
  • lime leaf
