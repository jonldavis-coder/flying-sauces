Chow-chow

Cabbage, green tomato, vinegar, and mustard: sharp, sweet, and crunchy.

25 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 12 · about 4 cups

History

Chow-chow is a chopped Southern relish of cabbage, green tomato, onion, and pepper in vinegar, mustard, and turmeric. It is common in Appalachian and wider Southern pantries. The name’s origin is disputed, so this page does not repeat a folk etymology. This is a refrigerator relish, not a canning recipe.

Cultural background

It was a way to keep the last green tomatoes and cabbage. It goes with beans, field peas, and cold meat. Turmeric makes it yellow. Mustard makes it sharp.

Why it tastes this way

Vinegar and mustard preserve and sharpen the vegetables. Sugar keeps the pickle from being only sour. Turmeric colors it and adds a bitter edge. The vegetables stay crisp because they are salted and cooked briefly, not stewed to mush.

How it is made

Salted cabbage, green tomato, and pepper are folded into a simmered vinegar, mustard, and turmeric dressing.

Authentic touch

Salt the vegetables and drain them before they meet the vinegar. Cook the dressing until the raw mustard taste is gone, then add the vegetables only long enough to heat through.

How it is used

Eat it cold with beans, greens, or a ham biscuit.

Field notes

  • Green tomato and cabbage are the usual body.
  • This version is refrigerated. It is not a shelf-stable canning formula.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups chopped cabbage
  • 2 green tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 bell pepper, chopped
  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 1 cup cider vinegar
  • 1/3 cup sugar
  • 1 tablespoon mustard powder
  • 1 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1 teaspoon mustard seeds

Steps

  1. Toss the vegetables with the salt, rest 30 minutes, rinse, and drain.
  2. Simmer the vinegar, sugar, mustard powder, turmeric, and mustard seeds 5 minutes.
  3. Stir in the vegetables and cook 3 minutes.
  4. Cool.
  5. Refrigerate.

Refrigerate and eat within two weeks. This is not a canning recipe. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Some pots use more green tomato than cabbage.
  • A hotter chow-chow adds cayenne.

Pairings

  • beans
  • ham biscuits
  • cooked greens
