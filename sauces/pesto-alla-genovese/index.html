Pesto alla Genovese

This stop is on Mediterranean by balloon, traveled by hot air balloon.

Raw basil, garlic, pine nuts, cheese, and olive oil: green, sharp, and rich.

History

Pesto alla Genovese is the basil sauce of Liguria. The name comes from pestare, to pound. The modern form, with basil, garlic, pine nuts, salt, Ligurian olive oil, and a mix of Parmesan and a hard Sardinian or local sheep cheese, was fixed in Genoese cooking in the nineteenth century. A mortar gives a creamier sauce than a blender.

Cultural background

It is a Ligurian sauce for trofie, trenette, and green beans with potato, not a generic herb paste. Other Italian pestos, such as Trapanese, are different recipes.

Why it tastes this way

Raw basil, garlic, pine nuts, cheese, and Ligurian olive oil are the flavor. Heat is not part of it. Pounding bruises the basil less than a long blend, so the sauce stays green and sweet instead of dark and bitter.

How it is made

Basil is pounded with salt, garlic, and pine nuts, then cheese and olive oil are worked in. Heat is not part of the sauce.

Authentic touch

Pound or pulse briefly and stop while it is still bright. Toss it with pasta off the boil. Boiling pesto turns it dull.

How it is used

Toss with pasta and a little pasta water, or stir into minestrone. Do not cook the pesto on a hard boil or the basil turns dull.

Field notes

  • Classic Genoese pesto is pounded, which bruises the basil less harshly than a long blend.
  • Pine nuts are traditional. Walnuts make a different Ligurian sauce, salsa di noci.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups packed fresh basil leaves
  • 1 small garlic clove
  • 2 tablespoons pine nuts
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup extra-virgin olive oil
  • 1/4 cup finely grated Parmesan
  • 2 tablespoons finely grated pecorino

Steps

  1. Pound the garlic and salt to a paste, then the pine nuts.
  2. Add the basil in handfuls and pound to a coarse green paste.
  3. Stir in the cheeses.
  4. Stream in the oil until the sauce is thick and spoonable.
  5. Toss with hot pasta and a splash of pasta water. Do not boil the pesto.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pesto alla Trapanese uses tomato and almonds and is a separate Sicilian sauce.
  • A blender works if you keep it cold and do not overmix the basil into a dark paste.

Pairings

  • trofie
  • trenette with potatoes and green beans
  • minestrone
