Niter Kibbeh

Spiced clarified butter: aromatic, nutty, and savory.

10 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 12 · about 1 cup

History

Niter kibbeh is Ethiopian spiced clarified butter. Butter is melted with onion, garlic, ginger, and spices, then strained. It is the cooking fat and finishing sauce of many Ethiopian dishes. Besobela and koseret are the traditional herbs. If you cannot buy them, the butter will be simpler. Dried basil is not the same herb, and this recipe does not pretend it is.

Cultural background

A spoon finishes vegetables and stews. The spice is in the fat, so a little is enough. It keeps in the refrigerator longer than fresh butter because the milk solids are strained off.

Why it tastes this way

Butter simmered with onion, ginger, and spices, then strained, tastes aromatic because the flavor is in the fat. Without the traditional herbs the fat is simpler, and this recipe does not rename other herbs to match them.

How it is made

Butter melts gently with aromatics and spices, simmers, and is strained clear.

Authentic touch

Keep the heat low so the butter clarifies instead of browning. Strain it clear.

How it is used

Use it to finish lentils or vegetables, or as the fat for a stew.

Field notes

  • Niter kibbeh is both a cooking fat and a finishing sauce.
  • The traditional herbs are specific. This page does not rename basil as besobela.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup unsalted butter
  • 2 tablespoons minced onion
  • 1 garlic clove, minced
  • 1 teaspoon grated ginger
  • 1 teaspoon ground turmeric
  • 1/2 teaspoon ground cardamom
  • 1/2 teaspoon fenugreek seeds
  • 1 clove

Steps

  1. Melt the butter with the onion, garlic, ginger, and spices over low heat.
  2. Simmer very gently 20 minutes without browning.
  3. Strain out the solids.
  4. Cool the clear fat.
  5. Use it to finish stews and vegetables.

Refrigerate the strained fat and use it within a month. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Without besobela and koseret the fat is still spiced, and it is not the full traditional aroma.
  • A very high heat will brown the butter and make a different fat.
