Mutabbal

Smoked eggplant, tahini, lemon, and garlic: smoky and creamy.

History

Mutabbal is the Levantine dip of fire-roasted eggplant, tahini, lemon, and garlic. Baba ghanoush is a related name. Recipes and names shift by country: some baba ghanoush is lighter on tahini and finished with pomegranate. This page uses the tahini-forward mutabbal and does not pretend every household agrees on the name.

Cultural background

It is meze, eaten with bread. The smoke from roasting the eggplant on a flame is the flavor that an oven tray only partly copies.

Why it tastes this way

Eggplant charred over a flame tastes smoky. Tahini and lemon make it creamy and tart. An oven tray without char will not give the same smoke.

How it is made

Eggplant is charred, the flesh is drained, and it is beaten with tahini, lemon, and garlic.

Authentic touch

Char the eggplant until it collapses, then drain the flesh so the dip is not watery.

How it is used

Spread it on a plate, finish with olive oil, and eat it with bread.

Field notes

  • The smoke is the point. A boiled eggplant will not taste like mutabbal.
  • Naming varies across the Levant, so this page picks the tahini-forward version and says so.

Ingredients

  • 2 medium eggplants
  • 1/3 cup tahini
  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice
  • 1 garlic clove, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil

Steps

  1. Char the eggplants over a flame or under a broiler until collapsed. Cool and peel.
  2. Drain the flesh in a sieve 10 minutes.
  3. Beat it with the tahini, lemon, garlic, and salt.
  4. Spread it and finish with oil.
  5. Serve at room temperature.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pomegranate-and-less-tahini version is one common reading of baba ghanoush.
  • Yogurt instead of tahini is a different dip.

Pairings

  • pita
  • raw vegetables
  • grilled meat
