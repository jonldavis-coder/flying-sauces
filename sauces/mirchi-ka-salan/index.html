Mirchi ka Salan

Chile, peanut, sesame, and tamarind: nutty, tangy, and warm.

20 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 6 · about 4 cups

History

Mirchi ka salan is the Hyderabadi chile curry served with biryani. Long chiles simmer in a sauce of peanut, sesame, coconut, and tamarind. It is a side curry, not a chutney. The chiles are often mild and are the vegetable, while smaller hot chiles can raise the heat.

Cultural background

A Hyderabadi biryani spread includes this gravy so the meal is not only rice and meat. The nut paste is the body.

Why it tastes this way

Peanut, sesame, and coconut make a nutty sauce. Tamarind makes it tangy. The long chiles are the vegetable, cooked mild, which is why it can sit beside biryani without overwhelming it.

How it is made

A paste of peanut, sesame, and coconut is fried with spice, then tamarind and whole chiles simmer in it.

Authentic touch

Toast and grind the nuts and seeds. Simmer the chiles until they are tender in that paste.

How it is used

Serve it beside biryani.

Field notes

  • The chiles are cooked as the vegetable of the curry.
  • It is a biryani accompaniment in Hyderabad, not a breakfast chutney.

Ingredients

  • 8 long mild green chiles, slit
  • 3 tablespoons peanuts
  • 1 tablespoon sesame seeds
  • 2 tablespoons grated coconut
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1 tablespoon tamarind paste
  • 2 cups water
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Toast and grind the peanuts, sesame, and coconut.
  2. Fry the paste in the oil with the turmeric.
  3. Add the tamarind, water, salt, and chiles.
  4. Simmer until the chiles are tender and the sauce is thick, about 20 minutes.
  5. Serve with biryani.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Mild banana chiles keep it a side. Hot chiles make it a hotter curry.
  • Coconut is traditional in many pots.

Pairings

  • hyderabadi biryani
  • rice
