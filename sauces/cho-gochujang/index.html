Cho-gochujang

Gochujang, vinegar, and sugar: hot, sour, and a little sweet.

5 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1/2 cup

History

Gochujang is a Korean fermented paste of chile, grain, and meju that takes months to make. Cho-gochujang is the quick dipping sauce made from that finished paste by stirring in vinegar and sugar, often with sesame. This recipe does not ferment anything. It starts from gochujang that has already been fermented. It is a table sauce for raw fish, vegetables, and other bites, not a substitute for the long ferment.

Cultural background

The vinegar is the cho. It thins the paste so it can be dipped, and it cuts the sweetness. Ssam and hoe tables both use versions of this mix.

Why it tastes this way

Finished gochujang already tastes of fermented chile, grain, and salt. Vinegar makes it sour and loosens it. Sugar keeps the vinegar from tasting thin and sharp. Sesame oil rounds the heat. A short stir is the whole method, which is why it still tastes like the paste you started with, only brighter.

How it is made

Gochujang is stirred with vinegar, sugar, and sesame until it is a dipping sauce.

Authentic touch

Start with real fermented gochujang. Do not cook a quick paste of chile powder and sugar and call it the same thing. Stir the vinegar in a little at a time until it drips from a spoon.

How it is used

Use it as a dip for raw fish, vegetables, or grilled items. It is not a marinade that needs to be boiled.

Field notes

  • The ferment already happened in the gochujang. This mix does not replace those months.
  • Cho means vinegar. The sour note is the point of this version.

Ingredients

  • 3 tablespoons gochujang
  • 1 tablespoon rice vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon toasted sesame oil
  • 1 teaspoon toasted sesame seeds

Steps

  1. Stir the gochujang, vinegar, and sugar until the sugar dissolves.
  2. Add the sesame oil and seeds.
  3. Add a few drops of water if it is too thick to dip.
  4. Taste. Add vinegar if you want it sharper.
  5. Serve the same day.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A minced garlic clove is common.
  • More vinegar makes a thinner hoe dip. Less makes a thicker ssam sauce.

Pairings

  • raw fish
  • cucumber
  • lettuce wraps
  • rice
