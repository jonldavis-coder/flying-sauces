Mast-o-Khiar

Yogurt, cucumber, dried mint, and rose: cool, herbal, and fragrant.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 2 cups

History

Mast-o-khiar is the Persian yogurt and cucumber sauce, served with rice and kebabs. Dried mint is typical, and dried rose petals appear in many bowls. It is thicker than a soup and is a relative of cacık and tzatziki, with its own herbs. Raisins or walnuts are optional and are not in this version.

Cultural background

It is on the table to cool grilled meat and to eat with rice. The dried mint, bloomed for a moment in a drop of hot water or oil, smells different from fresh mint.

Why it tastes this way

Yogurt and cucumber taste cool next to grilled meat. Dried mint, and sometimes rose, smell different from fresh dill. The sauce is thick so it sits with rice.

How it is made

Cucumber is folded into thick yogurt with dried mint, salt, and a pinch of dried rose if you have it.

Authentic touch

Salt the cucumber and squeeze it. Bloom dried mint between your fingers so it is fragrant, not dusty.

How it is used

Serve it cold with chelo kebab or rice.

Field notes

  • Dried mint, not only fresh dill, is the usual Persian scent.
  • It is a cooling sauce next to grilled meat, which is why it is cold and thick.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups thick plain yogurt
  • 1 cucumber, diced and lightly salted
  • 1 teaspoon dried mint
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon dried rose petals, optional
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice

Steps

  1. Squeeze extra water from the salted cucumber.
  2. Stir it into the yogurt with the mint, salt, rose, and lemon.
  3. Rest 15 minutes.
  4. Taste for salt.
  5. Serve cold.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chopped raisins are a common sweet note.
  • Walnuts make it a different, nut-containing bowl.

Pairings

  • chelo kebab
  • rice
  • herbs
