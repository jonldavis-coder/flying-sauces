Joppiesaus

Next stop: Frietsaus · Netherlands

Mayonnaise, curry, onion, and a little sweetness: creamy and spiced.

10 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 1 cup

History

Joppiesaus is the Dutch snack-bar sauce of mayonnaise, curry spices, and onion. It was created in the 2000s by Jan van Oorschot and became a national fry sauce very quickly. The commercial sauce is a brand recipe. This is a home-style version of that flavor, not the factory formula.

Cultural background

It sits beside frietsaus at snack bars and on kapsalon and fries. The onion is blended in, so the sauce stays smooth.

Why it tastes this way

Onion and curry stirred into mayonnaise taste savory and lightly sweet. The onion is blended smooth, which is why the sauce tastes of onion without crunching. It is a recent Dutch fry sauce.

How it is made

Onion, curry powder, and a little sugar are blended into mayonnaise.

Authentic touch

Mince or blend the onion very fine. Chunks of raw onion are not the snack-bar texture.

How it is used

Serve it on fries or in a sandwich.

Field notes

  • Joppiesaus is a recent Dutch sauce, not an old regional gravy.
  • The home version can echo the flavor and is not the branded recipe.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup mayonnaise
  • 1 tablespoon minced onion
  • 1 teaspoon curry powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon tomato paste
  • 1 tablespoon water
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Blend or finely mash the onion with the curry, sugar, tomato, and water.
  2. Stir that paste into the mayonnaise.
  3. Salt lightly.
  4. Rest 20 minutes.
  5. Serve on fries.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Frietsaus is the plain thinned mayonnaise.
  • More curry powder makes it sharper and less like the mild snack-bar version.

Pairings

  • fries
  • kapsalon
  • fried snacks
