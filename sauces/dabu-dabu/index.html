Dabu-Dabu

Tomato, chile, shallot, and lime: fresh, hot, and sharp.

15 min prep · 2 min cook · Serves 4 · about 2 cups

History

Dabu-dabu is the raw tomato and chile sambal of Manado in North Sulawesi. Shallot, tomato, lime, and a lot of chile are mixed and often scalded with a little hot oil. It is served with grilled fish. Colo-colo, from Maluku, is a close cousin and is not given a separate page.

Cultural background

Minahasan cooking is known for heat. Dabu-dabu is the fresh relish that goes on the fish rather than a coconut curry.

Why it tastes this way

Raw tomato, shallot, and a lot of chile taste fresh and hot, like a salsa. A spoon of hot oil softens the shallot. It is Manado food for grilled fish, not a cooked sambal.

How it is made

Tomato, shallot, and chile are chopped and dressed with lime, salt, and a spoon of hot oil.

Authentic touch

Chop it. Do not cook the tomato down. Salt and lime it, then spoon it on hot fish.

How it is used

Spoon it over grilled fish.

Field notes

  • Dabu-dabu is a fresh salsa from Manado, not a cooked sambal.
  • The chile level is high on purpose.

Ingredients

  • 2 tomatoes, diced
  • 4 shallots, sliced
  • 4 bird’s eye chiles, sliced
  • 2 tablespoons lime juice
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Mix the tomato, shallot, chile, lime, and salt.
  2. Heat the oil until hot and pour it over.
  3. Rest 10 minutes.
  4. Serve with grilled fish.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Colo-colo adds basil or a slightly different chile mix.
  • A fully raw version without hot oil is also served.

Pairings

  • grilled fish
  • rice
  • water spinach
