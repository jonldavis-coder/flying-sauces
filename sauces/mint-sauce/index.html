Mint Sauce

Fresh mint, vinegar, and a little sugar: sharp, cool, and sweet.

10 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 1/2 cup

History

English mint sauce is chopped mint with vinegar and sugar, served with roast lamb. It is a table sauce, not a jelly, though mint jelly later became a substitute. The pairing of lamb and mint in England is documented in cookbooks by the eighteenth and nineteenth centuries.

Cultural background

It is Sunday roast food. The mint should be fresh spearmint. A vinegar-heavy sauce cuts the fat of the lamb. Mint jelly is sweeter and cooked.

Why it tastes this way

Chopped mint in vinegar, with a little sugar, tastes cool and sharp against fatty lamb. It is uncooked. Mint jelly is sweeter and cooked, and it is a different condiment.

How it is made

Mint is chopped with sugar and covered with hot vinegar so the leaves soften.

Authentic touch

Use spearmint, chop it fine, and cover it with hot vinegar. It should taste more sharp than sweet.

How it is used

Serve it cold or cool with roast lamb.

Field notes

  • Mint sauce is sharp. Mint jelly is a different condiment.
  • Spearmint, not peppermint, is the usual herb.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup finely chopped mint leaves
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 1/4 cup boiling water
  • 1/4 cup white wine vinegar
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Stir the mint and sugar with the boiling water.
  2. Add the vinegar and salt.
  3. Rest at least 20 minutes.
  4. Taste. It should be more sharp than sweet.
  5. Serve with lamb.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Mint jelly is the cooked, sweeter cousin.
  • A spoon of boiling water before the vinegar softens the leaves.

Pairings

  • roast lamb
  • new potatoes
  • peas
