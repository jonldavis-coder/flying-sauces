Coconut Chutney

Coconut, green chile, and curry leaf: fresh, mild, and aromatic.

15 min prep · 5 min cook · Serves 6 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Coconut chutney is the white chutney of South Indian breakfasts, served with dosa and idli. Fresh coconut is ground with green chile, ginger, and salt, then finished with a tadka of mustard seeds and curry leaves. Roasted chana dal is a common addition and is left out here.

Cultural background

A dosa plate without coconut chutney and sambar is incomplete in many homes. The tempering is poured on at the end so the curry leaves stay fragrant.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh coconut tastes sweet and mild. Green chile heats it, and a hot tempering of mustard seed and curry leaf perfumes the top. Roasted dal is left out here, so the coconut stays the main flavor.

How it is made

Coconut, chile, and ginger are blended with water, then hot oil, mustard seed, and curry leaf are poured over.

Authentic touch

Pour the hot tadka over the chutney just before serving. Stale curry leaves taste flat.

How it is used

Serve it with dosa or idli.

Field notes

  • The tadka of curry leaf and mustard seed is part of the chutney, not a garnish you can skip casually.
  • Fresh coconut, not sweetened coconut flakes, is the ingredient.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup grated fresh coconut
  • 1 green chile
  • 1/2 inch ginger
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 1 teaspoon oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon mustard seeds
  • 6 curry leaves

Steps

  1. Blend the coconut, chile, ginger, salt, and water until coarse.
  2. Heat the oil, pop the mustard seeds, and fry the curry leaves a few seconds.
  3. Pour the oil over the chutney.
  4. Stir.
  5. Serve the same day with dosa.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of yogurt makes a different, dairy chutney.
  • Roasted dal thickens some family recipes.

Pairings

  • idli
  • vada
