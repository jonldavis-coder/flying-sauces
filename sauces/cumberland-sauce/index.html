Cumberland Sauce

Red currant, port, orange, and mustard: sweet, sharp, and spiced.

History

Cumberland sauce is an English cold sauce for cold meats and game, made from red currant jelly, port, orange and lemon zest, and mustard. It is named for Cumberland, and nineteenth-century cookbooks give versions of it. It is a cousin of the sweet-sour fruit sauces of northern Europe.

Cultural background

It is Boxing Day and buffet food, with ham, cold game, and pâté. It is served cold, which sets it apart from a hot roast gravy.

Why it tastes this way

Red currant, port, orange zest, and mustard taste sweet, sour, and spiced. It is served cold with cold meat, so the flavors are set, not simmered on the plate. Blanching the zest removes bitterness.

How it is made

Zest is blanched, then simmered with port and jelly, and mustard is stirred in off the heat.

Authentic touch

Blanch the zest, then chill the sauce fully. It is not a hot gravy.

How it is used

Chill it and serve it with cold ham, venison, or pork pie.

Field notes

  • Cumberland sauce is served cold, with cold meat.
  • The zest is blanched so the sauce is not bitterly pithy.

Ingredients

  • Zest of 1 orange and 1 lemon, cut in fine strips
  • 1/2 cup port
  • 1/2 cup red currant jelly
  • 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
  • 1 tablespoon orange juice
  • Pinch of cayenne

Steps

  1. Boil the zest in water 2 minutes and drain it.
  2. Simmer the zest with the port and jelly until the jelly melts.
  3. Stir in the mustard, orange juice, and cayenne off the heat.
  4. Cool completely.
  5. Serve cold with ham or game.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Red currant jelly is traditional. Cranberry jelly is a different sauce.
  • A spoon of ginger is used in some household recipes.

Pairings

  • cold ham
  • game
  • pâté
