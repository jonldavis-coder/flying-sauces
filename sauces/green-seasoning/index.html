Trinidadian Green Seasoning

Culantro or cilantro, thyme, garlic, and pepper: herbal and savory.

History

Green seasoning is the Trinidadian herb marinade of culantro or bandhania, thyme, garlic, scallion, and pepper, blended and used on meat before it is stewed or grilled. Culantro, when you can get it, tastes stronger than cilantro. The blend is a seasoning base, not a table salsa.

Cultural background

Home cooks keep a jar and season meats with it the night before. Thyme and garlic are constant. The herb changes with the market.

Why it tastes this way

A large amount of allium and herb, salted, tastes savory and green. It is blended fine so it clings to meat. Culantro is punchier than cilantro if you use it.

How it is made

Herbs, garlic, scallion, thyme, and pepper are blended with a little water and salt.

Authentic touch

Blend it fine, salt it, and rub it on meat for several hours before cooking. If you only have cilantro, the seasoning will be milder.

How it is used

Use it as a marinade, then cook the meat.

Field notes

  • Culantro and cilantro are different herbs. Culantro is the one many Trinidadian recipes mean.
  • It is a marinade base, used before the pot, not only at the table.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup cilantro or culantro
  • 4 scallions
  • 6 garlic cloves
  • 6 sprigs thyme, leaves only
  • 1 bell pepper
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons water

Steps

  1. Blend everything to a coarse paste.
  2. Rub it on meat.
  3. Refrigerate the meat several hours.
  4. Cook the meat through.
  5. Keep unused seasoning cold and use it within a few days.

Cook any meat it has touched. Do not eat the used marinade raw.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Haitian epis is a related herb base.
  • A chile in the blend makes it a hotter marinade.

Pairings

  • stewed chicken
  • grilled fish
  • pelau
