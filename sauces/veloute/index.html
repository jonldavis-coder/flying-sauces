Velouté

Light stock and a blond roux: silky, savory, and pale.

10 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 6 · about 2 cups

History

Velouté is the stock-based white mother sauce of the French repertoire. Carême and later Escoffier distinguished it from béchamel by the liquid: chicken, veal, or fish stock instead of milk. Sauce suprême, a cream-finished chicken velouté, is the best-known daughter.

Cultural background

French sauce cookery treats velouté as a blank savory canvas. The stock decides the sauce: fish velouté for poached fish, chicken velouté for vegetables and poultry.

Why it tastes this way

It tastes of the stock you choose because stock, not milk, is the liquid. A blond roux keeps the flavor light. Chicken stock tastes of poultry. Fish stock tastes of the sea. They are not interchangeable.

How it is made

A blond roux is loosened with hot stock and simmered until nappé, then strained.

Authentic touch

Simmer and skim. The long, gentle cook is what removes the raw-flour taste without browning the sauce.

How it is used

Spoon it over poached chicken, steamed vegetables, or fish. Reduce it further with cream for sauce suprême.

Field notes

  • The name means velvety, which describes the texture once the starch has cooked out.
  • A fish velouté and a chicken velouté are the same method and should not be swapped casually.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 2 1/2 cups hot chicken stock
  • Salt
  • White pepper

Steps

  1. Melt the butter and stir in the flour. Cook the roux to a pale blond, about 2 minutes.
  2. Whisk in the hot stock until smooth.
  3. Simmer 15 minutes, skimming, until the sauce coats a spoon.
  4. Strain, then season with salt and white pepper.
  5. Keep it warm over the lowest heat, with a film of stock or butter on top if you are not using it at once.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Sauce suprême finishes chicken velouté with cream and a little butter.
  • Sauce vin blanc adds a white-wine reduction and fish stock.

Pairings

  • poached chicken
  • steamed sole
  • asparagus
  • veal blanquette
