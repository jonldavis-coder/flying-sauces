Tomato Gravy

Tomato, milk, and a soft roux: tangy, savory, and pale red.

5 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 4 · about 2 cups

History

Tomato gravy is an Appalachian and Southern breakfast and supper sauce: fat, flour, tomato, and milk, poured over biscuits. It is what cooks made when there was tomato and no meat for sausage gravy, or alongside it. Bacon fat is common. The tomato is the flavor, not a long marinara.

Cultural background

It belongs to the same biscuit table as sausage gravy and, in some houses, chocolate gravy. The tomato keeps it bright. The milk keeps it a gravy.

Why it tastes this way

A soft roux thickens milk, and tomato adds acid so the gravy is not only creamy. Bacon fat, when you use it, tastes salty and smoky. A short cook is why it still tastes of fresh or canned tomato rather than a reduced pasta sauce.

How it is made

Fat and flour make a roux, tomato is stirred in, then milk loosens it into a gravy.

Authentic touch

Cook the flour in the fat until it smells nutty but stays pale. Add tomato before the milk so the acid does not scorch the roux.

How it is used

Pour it over biscuits.

Field notes

  • It is a biscuit gravy, not a pasta sauce.
  • Tomato stands in when there is no sausage, and some tables want both.

Ingredients

  • 2 slices bacon, or 2 tablespoons butter
  • 2 tablespoons flour
  • 1 cup chopped tomato
  • 1 cup milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon black pepper

Steps

  1. Cook the bacon and leave the fat, or melt the butter.
  2. Stir in the flour and cook 1 minute.
  3. Add the tomato and stir until it softens.
  4. Whisk in the milk and simmer until thick.
  5. Season with salt and pepper and serve on biscuits.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Oil instead of bacon fat makes a different, meatless gravy.
  • A pinch of sugar is used when the tomato is very sharp.

Pairings

  • biscuits
  • breakfast
  • fried potatoes
