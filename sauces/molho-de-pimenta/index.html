Malagueta chile and vinegar: bright and very hot.

10 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 16 · about 1 cup

History

Brazilian pepper sauce is often malagueta chiles steeped in cachaça, vinegar, or oil, kept on the table for feijoada, fish, and rice. The steep is the method. A cooked ketchup-style sauce is different.

Cultural background

A bottle of conserved chiles is a pantry habit. The liquid becomes the sauce. The chiles stay in the bottle.

Why it tastes this way

Malagueta is small and hot. Vinegar pulls that heat into a thin sauce so you can dash it. Time in the jar makes it hotter and rounder.

How it is made

Fresh hot chiles steep in vinegar and salt.

Authentic touch

Cover sliced chiles with vinegar and salt and wait at least a day. Use the liquid and a chile or two.

How it is used

Dash it on feijoada, fish, or rice.

Field notes

  • The vinegar is the sauce. The chiles stay in the jar.
  • Malagueta is the usual chile.

Ingredients

  • 10 fresh malagueta or bird’s eye chiles, sliced
  • 3/4 cup white vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 garlic clove, sliced

Steps

  1. Pack the chiles and garlic in a jar.
  2. Cover with vinegar and salt.
  3. Rest at least one day in the refrigerator.
  4. Shake before using.
  5. Use a few drops.

Refrigerate and use within a month. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Cachaça instead of some of the vinegar is a common conserve.
  • Oil-covered chiles are a different preserve.

Pairings

  • feijoada
  • grilled fish
  • rice
