Svíčková Sauce

Root vegetables, cream, and a dumpling-friendly tang: sweet, savory, and smooth.

25 min prep · 2 hr cook · Serves 6 · about 4 cups

History

Svíčková is the Czech cream sauce served with marinated beef tenderloin and bread dumplings. Root vegetables, vinegar or lemon, and cream are blended into a pale sauce. It is a Sunday and celebration dish in Czech cooking. The beef is braised with the vegetables, and the sauce is the blended braising liquid.

Cultural background

Cranberry or lingonberry relish and a spoon of whipped cream are the usual plate mates. The sauce should be tangy, not only rich.

Why it tastes this way

Root vegetables cooked with beef and blended with cream taste sweet and savory. Vinegar or lemon keeps the cream from being flat. The sauce is the vegetables, not a separate brown gravy.

How it is made

Beef and root vegetables braise, then the vegetables and liquid are blended with cream and a little vinegar.

Authentic touch

Blend the vegetables and braising liquid, then add cream and vinegar. Taste for tang. A relish on the side is part of the plate.

How it is used

Slice the beef and nap it with the sauce. Serve with bread dumplings if you have them.

Field notes

  • The sauce is the blended vegetables from the braise, not a separate brown gravy.
  • A tart fruit relish on the side is part of the Czech plate.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 pounds beef roast
  • 2 carrots, sliced
  • 1 parsnip, sliced
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 2 cups beef stock
  • 1/2 cup heavy cream
  • 1 tablespoon vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 4 peppercorns

Steps

  1. Brown the beef in the butter and remove it. Soften the vegetables in the fat.
  2. Return the beef, add the stock, bay, and peppercorns, and braise covered until tender, about 1 1/2 to 2 hours.
  3. Remove the meat. Discard the bay. Blend the vegetables and liquid.
  4. Stir in the cream and vinegar and simmer 5 minutes.
  5. Slice the beef and serve it with the sauce.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Lemon can replace the vinegar.
  • A dairy-free version is a different sauce. Cream is part of this one.

Pairings

  • beef tenderloin
  • bread dumplings
  • cranberry relish
