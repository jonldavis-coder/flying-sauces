Nam Prik Pao

Roasted chile, shallot, garlic, and shrimp paste, fried in oil: smoky and sweet.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 12 · about 1 cup

History

Nam prik pao is Thai roasted chile jam. Dried chiles, shallots, and garlic are roasted or fried, pounded with shrimp paste and palm sugar, and cooked in oil until jammy. It is a pantry sauce for tom yum, fried rice, and noodles. It is not a fresh nam prik relish.

Cultural background

A jar of it seasons a pot more often than it is eaten by the spoon alone. The sweetness of the palm sugar is part of the flavor, not a mistake.

Why it tastes this way

Roasted chile, shallot, and garlic fried with shrimp paste and palm sugar taste smoky and sweet. The jammy texture is oil and sugar. It seasons other dishes. It is not a fresh relish.

How it is made

Chiles, shallots, and garlic are darkened, pounded with shrimp paste and sugar, and fried in oil.

Authentic touch

Darken the shallots and garlic before you pound them, then fry the paste in oil until it looks like jam.

How it is used

Stir a spoonful into noodles, fried rice, or a soup.

Field notes

  • Pao means roasted, which is how the chile and alliums are cooked.
  • It is a jam-like pantry sauce, not a fresh table relish.

Ingredients

  • 8 dried red chiles
  • 4 shallots, unpeeled
  • 6 garlic cloves, unpeeled
  • 1 teaspoon shrimp paste
  • 1 tablespoon palm or brown sugar
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1 teaspoon fish sauce

Steps

  1. Dry-roast the chiles, shallots, and garlic until darkened. Peel the shallots and garlic.
  2. Pound them with the shrimp paste and sugar.
  3. Fry the paste in the oil until it looks like a dark jam.
  4. Stir in the fish sauce.
  5. Cool and refrigerate.

Refrigerate and use within a month. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A vegetarian roast-chile jam without shrimp paste is a different condiment.
  • More sugar makes it jammier and milder.

Pairings

  • fried rice
  • noodles
  • tom yum
