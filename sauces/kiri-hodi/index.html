Kiri Hodi

Coconut milk, turmeric, and curry leaf: mild, savory, and fragrant.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 3 cups

History

Kiri hodi is the Sri Lankan white coconut gravy, mildly spiced with turmeric, fenugreek, and curry leaf, often with onion and a little Maldive fish or fish sauce. It is the gentle curry next to hotter ones on a rice-and-curry plate. This version uses fish sauce.

Cultural background

It is comfort food, eaten with rice and with string hoppers. The coconut milk should not boil hard or it can split.

Why it tastes this way

Coconut milk, turmeric, and curry leaf taste mild and fragrant. A little fish sauce adds savor. It is the gentle gravy on a plate that also holds hotter curries.

How it is made

Onion, curry leaf, and turmeric simmer in thin coconut milk, then thicker coconut milk is added off a hard boil.

Authentic touch

Heat the coconut milk gently. A hard boil can split it.

How it is used

Serve it with rice and a hotter sambol.

Field notes

  • Kiri hodi is the mild coconut gravy on a plate that also holds hotter curries.
  • A hard boil can split the coconut milk.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 8 curry leaves
  • 1/2 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1/2 teaspoon fenugreek seeds
  • 1 can (14 ounces) coconut milk
  • 1 teaspoon fish sauce
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Fry the onion, curry leaves, turmeric, and fenugreek in the oil.
  2. Add half the coconut milk and simmer 8 minutes.
  3. Add the rest of the coconut milk, fish sauce, and salt.
  4. Heat gently. Do not boil hard.
  5. Serve with rice.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A boiled egg or a piece of fish can cook in the gravy.
  • Skip the fish sauce only if you replace the salt another way. The traditional savor often includes dried fish.

Pairings

  • rice
  • string hoppers
  • boiled eggs
