Monkey Gland Sauce

Tomato, onion, chutney, and Worcestershire: sweet, tangy, and savory.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · about 2 cups

History

Monkey gland sauce is a South African steakhouse sauce. It contains no monkey and no gland. The name is the one restaurants have used for a tomato, onion, fruit-chutney, and Worcestershire sauce since the mid-twentieth century. It is a pour-over for grilled steak, especially in Johannesburg dining rooms.

Cultural background

It is a steak sauce in the same social role as pepper sauce or mushroom sauce on a grill-house menu. The chutney is what makes it sweet in a South African way.

Why it tastes this way

Tomato, fruit chutney, onion, and Worcestershire taste sweet, tangy, and savory. The chutney is the South African sweetness. The name is a steakhouse label. There is no meat of that kind in the pot.

How it is made

Onion softens, then ketchup, chutney, vinegar, and Worcestershire simmer into a thick sauce.

Authentic touch

Simmer until thick and spoon it on grilled steak. Do not treat the name as an ingredient.

How it is used

Spoon it over grilled steak.

Field notes

  • The sauce contains no monkey. The name is a steakhouse oddity, not an ingredient list.
  • Fruit chutney is the characteristic sweetness.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 onion, minced
  • 1 cup ketchup
  • 1/4 cup fruit chutney
  • 2 tablespoons Worcestershire sauce
  • 1 tablespoon vinegar
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup water

Steps

  1. Cook the onion in the oil until soft.
  2. Add the ketchup, chutney, Worcestershire, vinegar, salt, and water.
  3. Simmer 12 minutes until thick.
  4. Blend if you want it smooth.
  5. Serve with steak.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Fruit chutney is the usual sweetener. A plain sugar sauce is less right.
  • More chile makes it hotter than the classic steakhouse pour.

Pairings

  • grilled steak
  • fries
  • pap
