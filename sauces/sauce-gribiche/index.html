Sauce Gribiche

Egg yolk, mustard, oil, capers, and herbs: sharp, chunky, and savory.

20 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 6 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Sauce gribiche is a cold French sauce built on cooked egg yolk, mustard, and oil, finished with capers, cornichons, and herbs. It is an older bistro sauce for tête de veau, cold fish, and asparagus. It looks like a loose mayonnaise and uses hard-boiled yolk, not raw.

Cultural background

It is a sauce for boiled and cold foods. The chopped whites are part of the texture. Mayonnaise with pickles stirred in is rémoulade, a cousin.

Why it tastes this way

Hard-boiled yolk, mustard, capers, and herbs taste sharp and chunky. The cooked yolk is why it does not taste like raw mayonnaise. Capers and cornichons are the acidity.

How it is made

Cooked yolks are mashed with mustard and vinegar, oil is worked in, and the whites, capers, cornichons, and herbs are folded through.

Authentic touch

Mash the yolks smooth before the oil. Chop the whites, capers, and pickles small so every bite has them.

How it is used

Serve it with cold fish, asparagus, or boiled meats.

Field notes

  • The yolk is hard-boiled, so this is not a raw-egg mayonnaise.
  • Capers and cornichons are the sharpness, not extra vinegar alone.

Ingredients

  • 2 hard-boiled eggs
  • 1 teaspoon Dijon mustard
  • 1 tablespoon white wine vinegar
  • 1/2 cup olive oil
  • 1 tablespoon capers
  • 2 cornichons, chopped
  • 1 tablespoon chopped tarragon and parsley
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Mash the yolks with mustard and vinegar.
  2. Whisk in the oil until creamy.
  3. Chop the whites and fold them in with the capers, cornichons, and herbs.
  4. Salt to taste.
  5. Serve cold.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Rémoulade uses raw-yolk mayonnaise as the base.
  • Tarragon is the herb most cooks expect.

Pairings

  • asparagus
  • leeks
  • cold poached fish
  • tête de veau
