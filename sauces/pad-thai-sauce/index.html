Pad Thai Sauce

Tamarind, fish sauce, and palm sugar: sour, salty, and sweet.

5 min prep · 8 min cook · Serves 4 · about 3/4 cup

History

Pad thai sauce is the seasoning for the fried noodle dish promoted in Thailand in the mid-twentieth century. Tamarind, fish sauce, and palm sugar are the core. The noodles, shrimp, tofu, and egg are the dish. The sauce is the sweet-sour balance that makes the noodles taste like pad thai rather than a generic soy fry.

Cultural background

Street cooks mix the sauce ahead so the wok moves fast. Chile and peanuts are added at the table or the edge of the plate, not always in the sauce itself.

Why it tastes this way

Tamarind is sour, palm sugar is sweet, and fish sauce is salty. That trio is the pad thai taste. Soy is not the main salt. The noodles are mild, so the sauce is concentrated.

How it is made

Tamarind, palm sugar, and fish sauce are simmered until the sugar dissolves and the sauce is syrupy.

Authentic touch

Mix the sauce before you cook the noodles. Taste it. All three notes should be obvious.

How it is used

Fry soaked rice noodles in a hot wok and add the sauce near the end.

Field notes

  • The sauce is tamarind, palm sugar, and fish sauce. Soy is not the main salt.
  • Pad thai as a national noodle was encouraged in the twentieth century. It is not an ancient court dish.

Ingredients

  • 1/4 cup tamarind paste
  • 1/4 cup palm or brown sugar
  • 3 tablespoons fish sauce
  • 2 tablespoons water

Steps

  1. Simmer all the ingredients until the sugar dissolves.
  2. The sauce should taste sour, salty, and sweet.
  3. Fry noodles in a hot wok.
  4. Add a few tablespoons of sauce and toss.
  5. Serve with lime.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A vegetarian sauce uses salt instead of fish sauce and is a different seasoning.
  • Chile on the side is traditional for heat.

Pairings

  • rice noodles
  • shrimp
  • bean sprouts
  • lime
