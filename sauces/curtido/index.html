Curtido

Cabbage, oregano, and vinegar: sharp, crunchy, and lightly herbal.

History

Curtido is the Salvadoran cabbage relish served with pupusas. Cabbage, carrot, onion, oregano, and vinegar are the usual pieces. It is a quick pickle, not a long ferment, in many home kitchens. The vinegar and oregano cut the richness of a cheese-filled pupusa.

Cultural background

A pupusa plate without curtido and a tomato sauce is unfinished. The slaw should be crisp. Oregano is the herb that makes it taste Salvadoran rather than like a generic coleslaw.

Why it tastes this way

Vinegar pickles the cabbage quickly, so it tastes sharp. Dried oregano perfumes it. Salt draws water out and keeps the crunch. A sweet mayonnaise slaw is a different food.

How it is made

Cabbage, carrot, and onion are salted, then dressed with vinegar and oregano.

Authentic touch

Toss the cabbage with salt, squeeze it, then dress it with vinegar and oregano. Serve it cold and crisp.

How it is used

Eat it on pupusas.

Field notes

  • Oregano, not mayonnaise, is the usual dressing note.
  • It is the relish for pupusas.

Ingredients

  • 4 cups shredded cabbage
  • 1 carrot, grated
  • 1/2 onion, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/3 cup white vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon sugar

Steps

  1. Toss the cabbage, carrot, and onion with salt. Rest 15 minutes and squeeze.
  2. Stir in oregano, vinegar, and sugar.
  3. Rest 20 minutes more.
  4. Taste.
  5. Serve cold with pupusas.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A longer brine softens it more.
  • Chile can be added. It is not required.

Pairings

  • pupusas
  • tomato sauce
  • beans
