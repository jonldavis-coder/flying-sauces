Piccalilli

Mustard, turmeric, vinegar, and pickled vegetables: sharp, yellow, and crunchy.

25 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 12 · about 4 cups

History

British piccalilli is a mustard pickle of cauliflower, onion, and other vegetables in a turmeric-yellow vinegar sauce. English cookbooks have versions from the eighteenth century onward, adapted from Indian pickles into a British accompaniment for cold meat and cheese. It is not a single Indian achar. This is a refrigerator pickle, not a shelf-stable canning recipe.

Cultural background

It is ploughman’s lunch food, with cheddar and ham. The sauce should be thick enough to cling to the vegetables and sharp enough to notice.

Why it tastes this way

Mustard and turmeric in vinegar taste sharp and yellow. The vegetables stay slightly crisp because they are salted first and cooked briefly. It is a British reading of Indian pickles, built for cold meat and cheese.

How it is made

Salted vegetables are simmered briefly in a vinegar sauce thickened with mustard and a little flour.

Authentic touch

Salt and rinse the vegetables so they stay crisp and not watery. Cook the mustard sauce until the flour taste is gone, then add the vegetables only briefly.

How it is used

Eat it cold with ham, pork pie, or cheese.

Field notes

  • The yellow is turmeric, the same way American mustard is yellow.
  • British piccalilli is a local reading of Indian pickles, not a copy of one achar.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 small cauliflower, in florets
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 tablespoon salt
  • 1 cup cider vinegar
  • 1/4 cup sugar
  • 1 tablespoon mustard powder
  • 1 teaspoon turmeric
  • 1 tablespoon flour
  • 2 tablespoons water

Steps

  1. Toss the vegetables with the salt, rest 30 minutes, rinse, and drain.
  2. Simmer the vinegar and sugar.
  3. Whisk the mustard, turmeric, flour, and water, and stir that into the vinegar.
  4. Simmer until thick.
  5. Stir in the vegetables, cook 2 minutes, cool, and refrigerate.

Refrigerate and eat within two weeks. This is not a canning recipe. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Green beans or cucumber can join the cauliflower.
  • A hotter English mustard powder makes it fiercer.

Pairings

  • cheddar
  • ham
  • pork pie
