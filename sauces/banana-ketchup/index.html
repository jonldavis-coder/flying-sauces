Banana Ketchup

Banana, vinegar, and spice, colored red: sweet, tangy, and smooth.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 12 · about 2 cups

History

Banana ketchup is a Filipino condiment developed in the twentieth century, associated with food scientist Maria Orosa’s work on local ingredients during shortages of tomato products. Ripe banana, vinegar, sugar, and spices are cooked and traditionally colored red. It became a everyday sauce for fried chicken, omelets, and spaghetti in the Filipino style.

Cultural background

It is sweeter than tomato ketchup. Filipino spaghetti sauce often uses it. This home version can stay pale if you skip food coloring. The flavor does not depend on the dye.

Why it tastes this way

Ripe banana cooked with vinegar and spices tastes sweet and tangy. Tomato is scarce in the origin story of the condiment. The red color of bottles is added. The banana is the flavor.

How it is made

Ripe bananas, vinegar, sugar, and spices simmer and are blended smooth.

Authentic touch

Use very ripe bananas and blend the sauce smooth. Tomato paste here is only for color and a little acidity.

How it is used

Use it on fried meats, eggs, or as the sweet base of a Filipino-style spaghetti sauce.

Field notes

  • The red color of bottled banana ketchup is added. The banana itself is not red.
  • Maria Orosa’s food work in the 1940s is the history most often tied to the condiment.

Ingredients

  • 3 very ripe bananas
  • 1/2 cup vinegar
  • 1/4 cup brown sugar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon garlic powder
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground clove
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste

Steps

  1. Simmer all the ingredients 10 minutes.
  2. Blend until smooth.
  3. Simmer 5 minutes more until thick.
  4. Cool.
  5. Refrigerate and use within two weeks.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of tomato paste makes it redder without commercial dye.
  • Tomato ketchup is a different fruit sauce.

Pairings

  • fried chicken
  • omelets
  • hot dogs
