Cacık

Yogurt, cucumber, garlic, and mint: cool, loose, and sharp.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 4 · about 2 1/2 cups

History

Cacık is the Turkish yogurt and cucumber dish, eaten as a cold soup or a thick sauce depending on how much water is added. Garlic and dried mint are typical. It is a relative of Greek tzatziki and is usually looser and more mint-forward. Both are written here because cooks treat them as named sauces of their own tables.

Cultural background

It is summer food beside grilled meat and pilaf, and a mezze spooned onto bread. Dried mint is the scent many Turkish kitchens expect.

Why it tastes this way

Yogurt, cucumber, garlic, and dried mint taste cool. Extra water makes it looser than tzatziki, sometimes soupy. Dried mint is the scent, different from fresh dill.

How it is made

Cucumber is grated into yogurt with garlic, dried mint, salt, and a little cold water.

Authentic touch

Rub the dried mint between your fingers as you add it so it smells fragrant. Add water only until it is as loose as you want.

How it is used

Serve it cold beside kebabs or as a light soup if you add more water.

Field notes

  • Cacık can be a sauce or a soup. The water is the switch.
  • Dried mint, not only fresh dill, is the usual herb.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups plain yogurt
  • 1 cucumber, grated and squeezed
  • 1 garlic clove, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried mint
  • 1/4 cup cold water
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Stir the yogurt, cucumber, garlic, mint, and salt.
  2. Add the water until it is saucy but not watery.
  3. Rest 10 minutes.
  4. Taste for salt.
  5. Serve cold.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Tzatziki is thicker and often uses fresh dill.
  • A handful of ice water makes cacık a drinkable soup.

Pairings

  • kebabs
  • pilaf
  • bread
