Donair Sauce

Sweetened condensed milk, vinegar, and garlic: sweet, tangy, and creamy.

10 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Donair sauce is the sweet garlic sauce of the Halifax donair, a spit-roasted beef wrap adapted from the Greek gyro by Peter Gamoulakos in the 1970s. Condensed milk, vinegar, and garlic are the usual sauce. It is sweeter than tzatziki.

Cultural background

The donair is a late-night Halifax food. The sauce is thick and sweet. Garlic keeps it from being only candy. It is not a yogurt sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Condensed milk is very sweet. Vinegar cuts it and thickens the mixture as the acid meets the milk. Garlic is the savory note. That sweet-sour dairy taste is the Halifax signature.

How it is made

Sweetened condensed milk, vinegar, and garlic are whisked into a thick sauce.

Authentic touch

Whisk vinegar into condensed milk slowly until it thickens, then add garlic. Let it rest so the garlic softens.

How it is used

Spread it in a donair wrap or serve it with the sliced meat.

Field notes

  • The Halifax donair dates to the 1970s.
  • The sauce is condensed milk and vinegar, not yogurt.

Ingredients

  • 1 can (14 ounces) sweetened condensed milk
  • 1/4 cup white vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon garlic powder
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Whisk the vinegar into the condensed milk until thick.
  2. Stir in the garlic and salt.
  3. Rest 20 minutes.
  4. Taste. Add vinegar if it is only sweet.
  5. Refrigerate.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • More garlic makes it sharper.
  • Tzatziki is the yogurt sauce of a gyro and a different taste.

Pairings

  • donair meat
  • pita
  • tomato and onion
