Bread Sauce

Milk, bread crumbs, onion, and clove: mild, peppery, and thick.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 6 · about 2 cups

History

English bread sauce is medieval in idea: bread thickened a sauce long before flour roux was standard. The version served with roast chicken, turkey, and game is milk infused with onion and clove, thickened with fresh crumbs, and finished with butter. It is a Christmas and Sunday roast sauce.

Cultural background

It sits beside cranberry sauce and gravy on a British Christmas plate, and beside roast chicken the rest of the year. The texture should be soft, not gluey.

Why it tastes this way

Milk infused with onion and clove, thickened with bread, tastes mild and spiced. Bread was a thickener before roux was common. The clove must perfume the milk and then come out, or the sauce tastes bitter.

How it is made

Milk is infused with onion and clove, the aromatics are removed, and bread crumbs thicken the milk. Butter finishes it.

Authentic touch

Steep the clove-studded onion and remove it. Stir in fresh crumbs and stop when the sauce is soft, not gluey.

How it is used

Serve warm with roast poultry or game.

Field notes

  • Bread sauce is older in spirit than roux-thickened gravies.
  • The clove should perfume the milk and then come out, or the sauce tastes bitter.

Ingredients

  • 2 cups whole milk
  • 1 small onion, peeled and studded with 3 cloves
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 cup fresh white bread crumbs
  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • Salt
  • White pepper

Steps

  1. Heat the milk with the onion, cloves, and bay until steaming. Steep 10 minutes off the heat.
  2. Remove the onion and bay.
  3. Stir in the crumbs and simmer until thick, about 5 minutes.
  4. Stir in the butter, salt, and pepper.
  5. Serve warm.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A bay leaf can join the clove.
  • Cream at the end makes it richer.

Pairings

  • roast chicken
  • roast turkey
  • game birds
