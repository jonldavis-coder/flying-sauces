Pikliz

Cabbage, carrot, scotch bonnet, and vinegar: sharp, hot, and crunchy.

20 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 10 · about 4 cups

History

Pikliz is the Haitian pickled slaw of cabbage, carrot, onion, and scotch bonnet in vinegar. It is the sharp relish next to fried pork, plantains, and rice. The vinegar preserves the crunch and the chile perfumes the jar.

Cultural background

A spoonful cuts rich fried food. It is ready after a rest in vinegar, not after a long canning process. This is a refrigerator pickle.

Why it tastes this way

Vinegar and salt pickle the vegetables so they stay crisp and taste sharp. Scotch bonnet steeps into the vinegar, which is why even the cabbage tastes hot.

How it is made

Cabbage, carrot, and chile are covered with vinegar and salt and rested.

Authentic touch

Pack the vegetables in vinegar with a whole or sliced scotch bonnet and wait at least a few hours, preferably a day.

How it is used

Eat it with griot, fried plantain, or rice.

Field notes

  • It is a vinegar slaw, not a mayonnaise slaw.
  • The chile seasons the whole jar as it sits.

Ingredients

  • 3 cups shredded cabbage
  • 1 carrot, julienned
  • 1/2 onion, sliced
  • 1 scotch bonnet, sliced
  • 1 cup white vinegar
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Pack the vegetables and chile in a jar.
  2. Stir the vinegar, water, and salt and pour them over.
  3. Press the vegetables down.
  4. Rest at least 4 hours in the refrigerator.
  5. Serve cold.

Refrigerate and eat within two weeks. This is not a canning recipe. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A boiled vinegar pour is a quicker pickle.
  • More chile makes a hotter jar.

Pairings

  • griot
  • fried plantain
  • rice
