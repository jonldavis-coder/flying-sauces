Sweden · Northern Europe

Hovmästarsås

Mustard, dill, sugar, and vinegar: sweet, sharp, and herbal.

10 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 3/4 cup

History

Hovmästarsås, the gravlax sauce, is the Swedish mustard-dill sauce served with cured salmon. The name means headwaiter’s sauce. It is a sweetened mustard sauce with lots of dill, not a cooked gravy. It became the standard partner for gravlax in the twentieth-century Swedish table.

Cultural background

It is Christmas and buffet food, next to gravlax, mustard, and bread. The sweetness is intentional and balances the salt of the cure.

Why it tastes this way

Sweet mustard, vinegar, and a large amount of dill balance salty cured salmon. The sugar is there because the fish is salty. Dill is a main ingredient, not a sprinkle.

How it is made

Mustard, vinegar, and sugar are whisked with oil into a loose emulsion, then dill is stirred in.

Authentic touch

Stir in a lot of fresh dill and taste it next to the salmon, not on its own. It should be sweet and sharp.

How it is used

Serve cold with gravlax or smoked salmon.

Field notes

  • The sauce is raw. The salmon is already cured.
  • Dill is not a garnish here. It is half the flavor.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons Swedish or Dijon mustard
  • 1 tablespoon white vinegar
  • 1 tablespoon sugar
  • 3 tablespoons neutral oil
  • 2 tablespoons chopped dill
  • Pinch of salt
  • Pinch of white pepper

Steps

  1. Stir the mustard, vinegar, and sugar until the sugar dissolves.
  2. Whisk in the oil.
  3. Stir in the dill, salt, and pepper.
  4. Rest 10 minutes.
  5. Serve with cured salmon.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A sharper sauce uses more Dijon and less sugar.
  • Honey can replace part of the sugar.

Pairings

  • gravlax
  • smoked salmon
  • boiled potatoes
