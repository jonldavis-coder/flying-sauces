Tkemali

Sour plum, garlic, and herbs: tart, fruity, and savory.

15 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 8 · about 2 cups

History

Tkemali is the Georgian sour-plum sauce, named for the small sour plums used to make it. Garlic, coriander, dill or pennyroyal, and chile are common. It is served with grilled meats and fried potatoes. Green and red versions follow the color of the plums. If true tkemali plums are unavailable, other sour plums are a stand-in and will taste less exact.

Cultural background

A Georgian grill plate often includes this tart sauce beside walnut sauces and pickles. The herbs are as important as the fruit.

Why it tastes this way

Sour plums taste tart, and garlic, chile, and herbs make that fruit savory. The tartness is the point next to grilled meat. Sugar would fight the meat.

How it is made

Sour plums are boiled, pitted, and simmered with garlic, herbs, and chile until thick.

Authentic touch

Use sour plums, not ripe sweet ones. Simmer until thick, and keep the herbs in the sauce, not only on top.

How it is used

Serve it with grilled meat or fried potatoes.

Field notes

  • The sauce is named for the plum.
  • It is tart on purpose. Sugar would fight the meat it is meant to cut.

Ingredients

  • 1 pound sour plums
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1/2 cup mixed cilantro and dill
  • 1/2 teaspoon coriander seed, ground
  • 1/2 teaspoon chile flakes
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Simmer the plums in the water until soft. Pit them and return the pulp to the pot.
  2. Add the garlic, herbs, coriander, chile, and salt.
  3. Simmer until thick, about 15 minutes.
  4. Blend if you want it smooth.
  5. Cool and serve with grilled meat.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Green plum tkemali is sharper. Red is a little rounder.
  • Pennyroyal is traditional. A mix of cilantro and dill is the practical herb base.

Pairings

  • fried potatoes
