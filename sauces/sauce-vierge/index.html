Sauce Vierge

Olive oil, tomato, basil, and lemon: fresh, fragrant, and loose.

10 min prep · 3 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1 cup

History

Sauce vierge is a warm or room-temperature olive-oil sauce of diced tomato, basil, and lemon or vinegar. It was popularized in French nouvelle cuisine, often credited to chef Michel Guérard in the 1970s. It is a modern French classic, not a nineteenth-century mother sauce.

Cultural background

It is the light sauce for grilled fish and summer vegetables. The oil is the body. There is no roux and no emulsion to hold for hours.

Why it tastes this way

Olive oil, diced tomato, basil, and lemon taste fresh because the tomato is not cooked down. Warming the oil wakes the garlic without frying the tomato into a sauce.

How it is made

Olive oil is warmed slightly with garlic, then tomato, basil, and lemon are stirred in off the heat.

Authentic touch

Seed the tomatoes so the water does not thin the oil. Tear the basil in at the end.

How it is used

Spoon it over grilled fish, scallops, or tomatoes.

Field notes

  • Vierge means virgin, referring to a light oil sauce rather than a butter or cream sauce.
  • It is a late-twentieth-century French sauce, not a medieval one.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup olive oil
  • 1 garlic clove, smashed
  • 2 tomatoes, seeded and diced
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 8 basil leaves, torn
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Warm the oil with the garlic until it smells fragrant. Do not brown it.
  2. Remove the garlic.
  3. Stir in the tomato, lemon, and salt off the heat.
  4. Add the basil.
  5. Spoon it over hot fish.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Coriander seed or fresh cilantro is a common restaurant twist.
  • A fully cold version is closer to a tomato vinaigrette.

Pairings

  • grilled sea bass
  • scallops
  • ripe tomatoes
