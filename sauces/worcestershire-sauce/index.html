Worcestershire Sauce

Anchovy, vinegar, molasses, and tamarind: salty, sour, and deep.

15 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 24 · about 1 cup

History

Worcestershire sauce was first sold by the chemists Lea and Perrins in Worcester in the 1830s. It is a fermented anchovy and vegetable sauce with vinegar, molasses, tamarind, and spices. The exact commercial formula is a trade secret. Tales about a forgotten barrel are company lore and are not repeated here as fact. This recipe is a simmered kitchen version of the flavor, not the aged original.

Cultural background

British cooking uses it in Welsh rarebit and with grilled meats. It also became a standard seasoning in cocktails and in kitchens far from Worcester. It is not a vegan sauce, because of the anchovy.

Why it tastes this way

Anchovy, vinegar, molasses, and tamarind taste salty, sour, and deep. The commercial sauce is fermented. This simmer captures the same family of flavors without the long age, so it will not taste identical to the bottle.

How it is made

Anchovy, onion, vinegar, molasses, tamarind, and spices simmer, then the sauce is strained. The result is a pantry approximation.

Authentic touch

Use it by the teaspoon. Reduce the simmered sauce until it is sharp. A weak mix tastes like sweet vinegar.

How it is used

Splash it into meat sauces, rarebit, or a marinade. A teaspoon is usually enough.

Field notes

  • Lea and Perrins of Worcester began selling the sauce in the 1830s.
  • The simmered home version is not the fermented factory sauce.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup white vinegar
  • 1/4 cup molasses
  • 2 tablespoons tamarind paste
  • 4 anchovy fillets
  • 1 small onion, chopped
  • 1 garlic clove
  • 1/4 teaspoon ground clove
  • 1/4 teaspoon chili powder
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Simmer all the ingredients 25 minutes.
  2. Blend and strain.
  3. Cool.
  4. Taste. It should be sharply savory, not sweet.
  5. Refrigerate. This is a kitchen version, not aged Worcestershire.

Refrigerate the strained sauce and use it within a month. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • The commercial sauce is fermented and aged. This simmered version is only a kitchen echo.
  • Soy sauce and anchovy together can stand in for a splash, and that is a shortcut, not this recipe.

Pairings

  • grilled steak
  • Welsh rarebit
  • Bloody Mary
