Caesar Dressing

Anchovy, garlic, egg, lemon, and Parmesan: savory and sharp.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1 cup

History

Caesar dressing was created by Caesar Cardini, an Italian-born restaurateur, at his restaurant in Tijuana in the 1920s. The salad is romaine, croutons, and Parmesan with a dressing of garlic, egg, lemon, and Worcestershire or anchovy. Early accounts differ on anchovy versus Worcestershire, which itself contains anchovy.

Cultural background

It is a tableside salad that became a bottled dressing. The savor is cured fish and cheese. The egg emulsifies the oil.

Why it tastes this way

Anchovy and Parmesan are the salt and the savor. Lemon is the acid. Egg yolk holds the oil. Without the fishy salt, a lemon mayonnaise does not taste like Caesar.

How it is made

Anchovy, garlic, yolk, lemon, and oil are emulsified, then cheese is added.

Authentic touch

Mash the anchovy and garlic into the yolk before the oil. Use real Parmesan. Toss the lettuce at the last minute.

How it is used

Toss it with romaine and croutons.

Field notes

  • Cardini’s Tijuana restaurant in the 1920s is the documented origin.
  • Anchovy and Worcestershire are both fish-based ways to get the savor.

Ingredients

  • 2 anchovy fillets
  • 1 garlic clove
  • 1 egg yolk
  • 1 tablespoon lemon juice
  • 1/3 cup olive oil
  • 2 tablespoons grated Parmesan
  • Pinch of black pepper

Steps

  1. Mash the anchovy and garlic.
  2. Whisk in the yolk and lemon.
  3. Stream in the oil.
  4. Stir in the cheese and pepper.
  5. Toss with romaine immediately.

The yolk is only lightly cooked if at all. Use a pasteurized egg if you want less risk, and keep the dressing cold.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Worcestershire instead of a visible anchovy is the other early path.
  • A coddled or pasteurized yolk is the safer modern choice.

Pairings

  • romaine
  • croutons
  • Parmesan
