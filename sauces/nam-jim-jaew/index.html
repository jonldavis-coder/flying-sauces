Nam Jim Jaew

Roasted chile, toasted rice, fish sauce, and lime: smoky, sour, and hot.

15 min prep · 8 min cook · Serves 4 · about 3/4 cup

History

Nam jim jaew is the Isan dipping sauce for grilled meat, especially gai yang and som tam’s grilled chicken. Toasted sticky-rice powder, roasted chile flakes, fish sauce, lime, and shallot or scallion are the usual pieces. The toasted rice is what makes it jaew rather than a plain nam jim.

Cultural background

It belongs with charcoal-grilled chicken and sticky rice. The sauce is thin enough to spoon, and the rice powder thickens it slightly as it sits.

Why it tastes this way

Toasted rice powder tastes nutty and slightly thickens the dip. Roasted chile is smoky. Lime and fish sauce are sour and salty. Without the toasted rice, it is just another nam jim.

How it is made

Rice is toasted and ground, chile is roasted, and both are stirred with lime and fish sauce.

Authentic touch

Toast the rice until golden and grind it. Stir it in just before serving so it thickens as it sits.

How it is used

Serve it with grilled chicken and sticky rice.

Field notes

  • Toasted rice powder is the ingredient that defines jaew.
  • It is an Isan sauce, built for grilled meat rather than for a coconut curry.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon sticky rice or raw rice
  • 1 tablespoon chile flakes
  • 2 tablespoons fish sauce
  • 2 tablespoons lime juice
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon soy sauce
  • 2 scallions, sliced

Steps

  1. Toast the rice in a dry pan until golden, then grind it.
  2. Toast the chile flakes a few seconds.
  3. Stir the fish sauce, lime, sugar, and soy.
  4. Add the rice powder, chile, and scallion.
  5. Serve with grilled chicken.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Shallot instead of scallion is common.
  • A gluten-free tamari-style fish-sauce-only version needs no soy. This one uses a splash of soy, which is why it is not tagged gluten-free.

Pairings

  • gai yang
  • sticky rice
  • grilled pork
