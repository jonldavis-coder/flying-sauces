Silsi

Onion, tomato, and berbere: savory, spiced, and lightly hot.

10 min prep · 30 min cook · Serves 4 · about 2 cups

History

Silsi is the spiced tomato and onion sauce of Eritrean and Tigrayan breakfasts, served with injera or bread and often with eggs. Onion is cooked down, berbere and tomato follow. It is a sauce you eat with bread, not a side relish.

Cultural background

Breakfast platters put silsi next to yogurt or salad and eggs. The onion should be sweet from a slow cook before the spices go in.

Why it tastes this way

Onion cooked slowly tastes sweet, then berbere and tomato make it savory and hot. That order is why breakfast silsi tastes round instead of like raw onion and raw spice.

How it is made

Onion cooks in oil until soft, then berbere, garlic, and tomato simmer into a thick sauce.

Authentic touch

Cook the onion soft before the berbere goes in. Serve it with injera or bread so the sauce has something mild to meet.

How it is used

Serve it with injera and a fried or scrambled egg.

Field notes

  • Silsi is a breakfast sauce, which is why it shows up with eggs.
  • The long onion cook is the base. Berbere goes in after the onion is soft.

Ingredients

  • 3 tablespoons oil
  • 2 onions, sliced
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon berbere
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/4 cup water

Steps

  1. Cook the onions in the oil until soft and sweet, about 15 minutes.
  2. Add the garlic and berbere and stir 1 minute.
  3. Add the tomatoes, salt, and water.
  4. Simmer until thick, about 12 minutes.
  5. Serve with injera and eggs.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A hotter berbere makes a fiercer breakfast.
  • Adding egg into the sauce makes a different skillet dish.

Pairings

  • injera
  • eggs
  • yogurt on the side
