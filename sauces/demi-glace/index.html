Demi-Glace

Brown stock reduced with espagnole: glossy, concentrated, and savory.

15 min prep · 1 hr 30 min cook · Serves 8 · about 1 cup

History

Demi-glace, in the Escoffier system, is espagnole combined with brown stock and reduced by about half until it is glossy and nappé. The name means half-glaze. A restaurant pan sauce that reduces a purchased stock is related in flavor and is not the full classic preparation.

Cultural background

It is a kitchen staple of French sauce work, used by the spoonful to finish meat sauces. It is not usually eaten by itself.

Why it tastes this way

The deep, glossy taste is reduction: water leaves, and browned stock concentrates. A little tomato and a brown roux add body. It tastes meaty because the liquid is brown stock, not because of a spice mix.

How it is made

Espagnole and brown stock simmer together until the sauce is reduced, shiny, and thick enough to coat a spoon. This home version builds a shorter brown sauce and reduces it.

Authentic touch

Reduce until it coats a spoon. If it tastes thin, it is not done. Salt only at the end, because reduction concentrates salt.

How it is used

Whisk a spoonful into pan juices for steak, short ribs, or a roast. A little goes a long way.

Field notes

  • Half-glaze refers to the reduction, not to a glaze made of sugar.
  • A boxed stock reduced with butter is a useful shortcut and a different sauce.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 1 carrot, chopped
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1/2 cup dry red wine
  • 4 cups unsalted beef stock
  • 1 tablespoon flour mixed with 1 tablespoon soft butter
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Brown the onion and carrot in the oil. Stir in the tomato paste and cook until it darkens.
  2. Add the wine and reduce by half.
  3. Add the stock and simmer until reduced to about 1 1/2 cups.
  4. Whisk in bits of the butter-flour paste and simmer until the sauce coats a spoon.
  5. Strain and season. The result is a home demi-glace, shorter than the classical reduction.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A wine reduction before the demi-glace goes in makes a simple pan sauce.
  • A fully classical demi-glace starts from a proper espagnole and a long brown stock.

Pairings

  • steak
  • short ribs
  • roasted game
  • mushroom pan sauce
