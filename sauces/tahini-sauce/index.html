Tahini Sauce

Tahini, lemon, and garlic, loosened with water: nutty and tart.

History

Tahini sauce is the Levantine sauce of sesame paste, lemon, garlic, and water. It is eaten with falafel, shawarma, fish, and roasted vegetables across Lebanon, Syria, Palestine, Jordan, and Israel. The water is what turns the stiff paste into a pourable sauce. It is not hummus, which adds chickpeas.

Cultural background

Falafel sandwiches are unfinished without it. The sauce lightens in color as the water goes in, which surprises people the first time.

Why it tastes this way

Sesame paste tastes bitter and thick until lemon and cold water loosen it and turn it pale. That water is why a good tahini sauce tastes light and tangy instead of like a spoon of paste.

How it is made

Tahini, lemon, and garlic are stirred, then cold water is added until the sauce loosens and turns pale.

Authentic touch

Stir lemon in first, even if the paste seizes, then add cold water a spoon at a time until it is pale and pourable.

How it is used

Drizzle it on falafel, roasted cauliflower, or fish.

Field notes

  • Tahini seizes and looks broken when lemon hits it. Water brings it back.
  • Hummus contains tahini sauce’s ingredients plus chickpeas.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup tahini
  • 1/4 cup lemon juice
  • 1 small garlic clove, minced
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1/3 cup cold water

Steps

  1. Stir the tahini, lemon, garlic, and salt. It will thicken.
  2. Add the water a spoon at a time until pale and pourable.
  3. Taste for salt and lemon.
  4. Rest 10 minutes.
  5. Serve on falafel or vegetables.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Yogurt makes a milder sauce and adds dairy.
  • Parsley stirred in is common and does not change the method.

Pairings

  • falafel
  • roasted cauliflower
  • fish
