Moambe Sauce

Palm fruit, onion, and chile: rich, earthy, and savory.

History

Moambe, or mwambe, is the palm-fruit sauce of the Congo, also called palm butter stew. The sauce is made from the pulp of the oil palm fruit, onion, and chile, and it simmers with chicken or other meat. Related palm sauces are cooked in Angola and around the Gulf of Guinea under other names.

Cultural background

It is celebration and home food, served with rice, plantain, or cassava. The orange-red color comes from the palm fruit.

Why it tastes this way

Palm fruit, not tomato, is the color and the rich taste. Onion and chile season it. The sauce thickens as the palm cream cooks, which is why it coats chicken and rice.

How it is made

Canned or jarred palm soup base is simmered with onion, garlic, chile, and stock until it becomes a thick sauce.

Authentic touch

Simmer gently. A hard boil can split palm cream into grease.

How it is used

Serve with chicken that has cooked in the sauce, plus rice or fried plantain.

Field notes

  • The color comes from palm fruit, not from tomato alone.
  • Use a prepared palm base. This page is not a guide to processing raw palm fruit.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 scotch bonnet, minced
  • 1 can (14 ounces) palm soup base or palm butter
  • 2 cups chicken stock
  • Salt
  • 1 pound chicken pieces

Steps

  1. Brown the chicken in the oil and set it aside. Fry the onion, garlic, and chile.
  2. Stir in the palm base and stock.
  3. Return the chicken and simmer until cooked through and the sauce is thick, about 35 minutes.
  4. Salt to taste.
  5. Serve with rice.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Nigerian ofe banga is a Delta palm-fruit soup with its own spices.
  • Ivorian sauce graine is a related palm sauce.

Pairings

  • chicken
  • rice
  • fried plantain
