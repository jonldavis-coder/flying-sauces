Sauce Graine

Palm nut, onion, and chile: rich, earthy, and mildly hot.

15 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 6 · about 5 cups

History

Sauce graine is the Ivorian palm-nut sauce, served with rice, foutou, or alloco. Graine means the palm fruit. The sauce is cooked across the country with fish, bush meat, or chicken. It is related to other Gulf of Guinea palm sauces and seasoned in an Ivorian way, often with tomato and chile.

Cultural background

It is everyday and celebration food. The palm base is sold prepared. This page uses that prepared base rather than fresh fruit processing.

Why it tastes this way

Palm nut is the rich, earthy taste. Tomato and chile are the Ivorian seasoning on top of that fruit. It is related to other palm sauces and seasoned differently.

How it is made

Onion and tomato fry, palm cream and stock are added, and meat or fish simmers until the sauce thickens.

Authentic touch

Use prepared palm cream and cook it with the tomato until thick. Do not process fresh palm fruit from this page.

How it is used

Serve it with rice or foutou.

Field notes

  • Graine in this sauce name means palm nut, not a generic seed.
  • Use a prepared palm base. This is not a lesson in processing fresh palm fruit.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 2 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1 scotch bonnet, minced
  • 1 can (14 ounces) palm soup base
  • 2 cups chicken stock
  • Salt
  • 1 pound chicken pieces

Steps

  1. Brown the chicken and set it aside. Fry the onion, tomato, and chile.
  2. Stir in the palm base and stock.
  3. Return the chicken and simmer until cooked and the sauce is thick.
  4. Salt to taste.
  5. Serve with rice.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A fish version is common on the coast.
  • Nigerian banga uses different spices and is its own page.

Pairings

  • rice
  • attieke
  • fried plantain
