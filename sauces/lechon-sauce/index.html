Lechon Sauce

Liver, vinegar, sugar, and bread crumbs: sweet, sharp, and savory.

10 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 8 · about 2 cups

History

Filipino lechon sauce, sometimes called sarsa, is the liver-based sauce served with roast pig. Vinegar, sugar, garlic, and breadcrumbs thicken a gravy that includes mashed liver. It is a feast sauce. The sweetness and the vinegar are both supposed to be obvious.

Cultural background

Lechon is celebration food. The sauce is spooned over the crackling and the meat. A liverless vinegar sauce exists and is a different, simpler dip.

Why it tastes this way

Liver mashed into vinegar and sugar tastes savory, sweet, and sharp. The vinegar cuts roast pork fat. Bread crumbs thicken it. A liverless dip is a different, lighter sauce.

How it is made

Garlic fries, liver is mashed in with vinegar, sugar, and stock, and bread crumbs thicken it.

Authentic touch

Cook the liver through and mash it smooth. Taste for both vinegar and sugar.

How it is used

Serve it with roast pork.

Field notes

  • The liver is what makes the classic sauce brown and savory.
  • Vinegar and sugar together are the Filipino sweet-sour line in this gravy.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 4 ounces chicken livers, chopped
  • 1/4 cup vinegar
  • 2 tablespoons brown sugar
  • 1 cup meat stock
  • 1/4 cup bread crumbs
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Fry the garlic in the oil.
  2. Add the livers and cook until no longer pink.
  3. Mash them into the oil.
  4. Add the vinegar, sugar, stock, crumbs, and salt and simmer 10 minutes.
  5. Serve with roast pork.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A vinegar-sugar-garlic dip without liver is the simpler table sauce.
  • More bread crumbs make it thicker.

Pairings

  • lechon
  • roast pork
  • rice
