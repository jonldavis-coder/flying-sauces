Balachaung

Fried dried shrimp, garlic, and chile: crisp, salty, and hot.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 8 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Balachaung is the Burmese fried condiment of dried shrimp, garlic, onion, and chile, cooked in oil until crisp. It is eaten with rice. The texture is a crunchy relish more than a smooth sauce. It keeps because it is fried and salty, and at home it should still be refrigerated once the jar is open.

Cultural background

A spoonful on plain rice is a meal’s seasoning. The garlic should be golden. Dark garlic turns the whole pot bitter.

Why it tastes this way

Dried shrimp, garlic, and onion fried until crisp taste salty, sweet, and hot. The crunch is the texture. A smooth chile jam is a different condiment. Golden garlic is the line. Dark garlic is bitter.

How it is made

Dried shrimp, garlic, and onion fry in oil with chile until crisp.

Authentic touch

Fry the shrimp and garlic separately to golden, then combine them. Cool the relish before you jar it.

How it is used

Sprinkle or spoon it over rice.

Field notes

  • Balachaung is crispy on purpose. It is not a smooth chile jam.
  • Dried shrimp, not fresh prawns, are the usual base.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup dried shrimp
  • 1/3 cup oil
  • 6 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1 small onion, sliced
  • 1 teaspoon chile flakes
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon fish sauce

Steps

  1. Rinse and dry the shrimp. Fry them in the oil until crisp and remove them.
  2. Fry the garlic and onion in the same oil until golden.
  3. Return the shrimp, add the chile, salt, and fish sauce.
  4. Cook 2 minutes more.
  5. Cool and serve over rice.

Cool and refrigerate. Use within two weeks. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A wetter, less fried version is served in some homes.
  • Ngapi, fish paste, is a different Burmese condiment.

Pairings

  • steamed rice
  • boiled vegetables
  • mangos
