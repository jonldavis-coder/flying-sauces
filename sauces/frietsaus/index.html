Frietsaus

Thinned mayonnaise: creamy, mild, and smooth.

5 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 1 cup

History

Frietsaus is the Dutch sauce for fries, a mayonnaise thinned so it can be dispensed easily. It is thinner and usually sweeter or milder than Belgian mayonnaise, which is the other classic fry sauce of the Low Countries. Snack bars have sold it for decades as the default dip.

Cultural background

It is snack-bar food, in a long dollop on fries. Joppiesaus is the onion-curry variant and is a different named sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Mayonnaise thinned with a little milk tastes milder and pours. Dutch snack bars need a sauce that flows onto fries. It is not as thick as Belgian mayonnaise.

How it is made

Mayonnaise is loosened with a little milk or water and seasoned lightly.

Authentic touch

Thin it until it pours. If it stands in a stiff peak, it is mayonnaise, not frietsaus.

How it is used

Serve it on fries. It should flow, not sit in a stiff peak.

Field notes

  • Frietsaus is thinned mayonnaise, which is why it pours from a snack-bar pump.
  • It is not the same texture as a spoon of homemade mayonnaise.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup mayonnaise
  • 2 tablespoons milk
  • 1/2 teaspoon sugar
  • Pinch of salt
  • Pinch of white pepper

Steps

  1. Stir the mayonnaise and milk until smooth and pourable.
  2. Add the sugar, salt, and pepper.
  3. Taste. It should be mild.
  4. Refrigerate.
  5. Serve on hot fries.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Belgian mayonnaise for fries is thicker and often richer.
  • Joppiesaus adds curry and onion.

Pairings

  • patat
  • bitterballen
  • broodje kroket
