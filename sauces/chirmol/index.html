Chirmol

Roasted tomato, onion, and chile: smoky, fresh, and hot.

15 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 6 · about 2 cups

History

Chirmol is a Guatemalan chopped salsa of roasted or raw tomato, onion, chile, and cilantro or mint, served with grilled meat. Roasting the tomatoes is a common path and gives smoke. It is a table salsa, related to other chopped salsas of the region and seasoned in Guatemalan kitchens.

Cultural background

It is spooned over grilled beef. Lime and salt finish it. The char on the tomato is the difference from a fully raw pico.

Why it tastes this way

Charred tomato tastes smoky and sweet. Raw onion and chile stay sharp. That mix is why chirmol tastes both roasted and fresh.

How it is made

Tomatoes are charred, then chopped with onion, chile, cilantro, lime, and salt.

Authentic touch

Char the tomatoes and chop them by hand with onion and chile. Do not puree it smooth.

How it is used

Serve it with grilled meat.

Field notes

  • Chopped and charred, not blended smooth, is the usual texture.
  • It is a grilled-meat salsa.

Ingredients

  • 4 tomatoes
  • 1/2 onion, finely chopped
  • 1 jalapeño, minced
  • 1/4 cup cilantro
  • 1 lime, juiced
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Char the tomatoes, peel if you wish, and chop them.
  2. Mix with onion, chile, cilantro, lime, and salt.
  3. Rest 10 minutes.
  4. Taste.
  5. Serve with grilled meat.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A fully raw chirmol is brighter.
  • Mint appears in some pots instead of cilantro.

Pairings

  • grilled beef
  • tortillas
  • black beans
