Sauce Bordelaise

Red wine, shallot, bone marrow, and demi-glace: dark, winey, and savory.

15 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1 cup

History

Sauce bordelaise is the Bordeaux red-wine sauce of French cookery: a reduction of red wine and shallot finished with demi-glace and, classically, poached beef marrow. The wine is the region’s signature. Escoffier and other nineteenth-century writers record it as a steak sauce.

Cultural background

It belongs with grilled beef. The marrow is traditional and easy to skip, which makes a simpler wine sauce rather than the full bordelaise.

Why it tastes this way

Red wine reduced with shallot is the flavor, backed by brown stock. Marrow, when you use it, adds a soft beef richness. The wine has to reduce or the sauce tastes thin and alcoholic.

How it is made

Shallot and red wine reduce, demi-glace is added, and the sauce is finished with butter and pieces of marrow.

Authentic touch

Reduce the wine until it is almost a syrup before the stock goes in. A short boil leaves it tasting like wine soup.

How it is used

Serve with grilled steak or roasted beef.

Field notes

  • The name is the wine region, and the wine in the pan is the point.
  • Marrow is the classical garnish, poached so it stays soft.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • 1 shallot, minced
  • 1 cup dry red Bordeaux or other dry red wine
  • 1 cup beef stock
  • 1 teaspoon tomato paste
  • 2 ounces beef marrow, diced, optional
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Cook the shallot in the butter until soft.
  2. Add the wine and reduce to about 1/4 cup.
  3. Add the stock and tomato paste and reduce until the sauce coats a spoon.
  4. Season. If you use marrow, poach the dice in simmering water 1 minute and add them.
  5. Serve with steak.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Without marrow it is a red-wine pan sauce, not a full bordelaise.
  • A splash of the steak’s resting juices can replace part of the stock.

Pairings

  • entrecôte
  • roasted marrow bones
  • mushrooms
