Onion Gravy

Slow onion and brown stock: sweet, dark, and savory.

10 min prep · 35 min cook · Serves 4 · about 3 cups

History

Onion gravy is the British sauce of slowly cooked onions, flour, and stock, served with sausages, toad-in-the-hole, and mash. It is weeknight food and pub food. The onions have to cook long enough to sweeten. A pale, rushed onion gravy tastes raw.

Cultural background

Bangers and mash are the plate most people mean. The same gravy belongs with liver and with a pie. Worcestershire is a common splash and adds anchovy if you use it. This version stays with stock.

Why it tastes this way

Onions cooked until deep gold taste sweet. Flour and stock turn that sweetness into a brown gravy for sausages. Rushed, pale onions taste raw and the gravy tastes floury.

How it is made

Onions cook slowly in fat until brown, flour is stirred in, and stock simmers the gravy thick.

Authentic touch

Give the onions the time to brown. That browning is the flavor. Deglaze with stock only after they are golden.

How it is used

Pour it over sausages and mashed potatoes.

Field notes

  • The onions need time. Browning them is the flavor.
  • It is a sausage gravy in the British sense, not the American milk-and-sausage gravy.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons butter or oil
  • 3 onions, thinly sliced
  • 1 tablespoon flour
  • 2 1/2 cups beef stock
  • 1 teaspoon vinegar
  • Salt
  • Black pepper

Steps

  1. Cook the onions in the fat over medium-low heat until deep golden, about 25 minutes.
  2. Stir in the flour for 1 minute.
  3. Add the stock and simmer 10 minutes.
  4. Add the vinegar, salt, and pepper.
  5. Serve with sausages.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of mustard sharpens it.
  • Beer can replace part of the stock.

Pairings

  • sausages
  • mashed potatoes
  • toad-in-the-hole
