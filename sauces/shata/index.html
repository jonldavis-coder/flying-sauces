Sudanese Shata

Fresh chile, garlic, and lemon: hot, sharp, and clean.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 8 · about 1 cup

History

Shata is the Sudanese chile sauce served with grilled meats, ful, and kisra. Green or red chiles are pounded with garlic, salt, and lemon or vinegar. Peanut versions exist in some kitchens. This recipe stays with chile, garlic, and lemon so it does not blur into a peanut sauce.

Cultural background

A spoonful beside grilled lamb is the usual place. It is fresh and hot, not a long-cooked ketchup.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh chile, garlic, and lemon taste hot and clean. This version leaves peanut out so the flavor stays chile and acid, which is the straight table sauce beside grilled meat.

How it is made

Chiles and garlic are pounded with salt and lemon into a loose relish.

Authentic touch

Pound it the day you eat it and salt it enough. A peanut version is a different sauce.

How it is used

Serve it with grilled meat or ful.

Field notes

  • Shata is a table chile sauce for grilled meat.
  • This version leaves peanut out so it stays a straight chile-garlic sauce.

Ingredients

  • 6 green chiles
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons water
  • 1 tablespoon oil

Steps

  1. Pound or blend the chiles, garlic, lemon, salt, and water.
  2. Stir in the oil.
  3. Rest 10 minutes.
  4. Taste for salt.
  5. Serve with grilled meat.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A peanut version is used by some cooks and is a different sauce.
  • Green chiles make the brighter shata.

Pairings

  • grilled lamb
  • ful
  • kisra
