Massaman Curry

The next field stop is a nearby sauce on the same map.

Warm spice, peanut, coconut, and tamarind: mild, sweet, and savory.

25 min prep · 1 hr 10 min cook · Serves 6 · about 6 cups

History

Massaman is a Thai Muslim-influenced curry of the south, with dried spices such as cinnamon, cardamom, and cumin alongside chile and shrimp paste. Potatoes and peanuts are typical. It is milder and sweeter than a plain red curry. The name is linked to the word Muslim in Thai food history.

Cultural background

Beef or chicken simmers until tender. Tamarind and palm sugar balance the coconut. It is a celebration and restaurant curry as much as a home one.

Why it tastes this way

Cinnamon, cumin, and cardamom taste warm and mild next to chile. Peanut and potato add body. Tamarind and palm sugar make it sour-sweet. That spice set is why it does not taste like a plain red curry.

How it is made

A spiced paste is fried, then coconut milk, meat, potato, peanut, tamarind, and sugar simmer until the meat is soft.

Authentic touch

Simmer until the meat is tender and the sauce is thick. Taste for tamarind. It should not be only sweet.

How it is used

Serve it with rice.

Field notes

  • Massaman is milder than green or red curry because warm spices share the stage with chile.
  • Potato in a Thai curry is a massaman habit, not a green-curry one.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 2 tablespoons red curry paste
  • 1 teaspoon ground cinnamon
  • 1 pound beef stew meat
  • 1 can (14 ounces) coconut milk
  • 1 potato, cubed
  • 1/4 cup roasted peanuts
  • 1 tablespoon tamarind paste
  • 1 tablespoon palm or brown sugar
  • 1 tablespoon fish sauce

Steps

  1. Fry the curry paste and cinnamon in the oil.
  2. Add the beef and brown it.
  3. Add the coconut milk and simmer until the beef is nearly tender, about 45 minutes.
  4. Add the potato, peanuts, tamarind, sugar, and fish sauce. Simmer until the potato is soft.
  5. Serve with rice.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chicken massaman is common.
  • Leaving out peanuts makes a different, nut-free sauce and is less typical.

Pairings

  • rice
  • potato
  • beef
