Avgolemono

Egg and lemon in broth: silky, tart, and savory.

10 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 4 · about 2 cups

History

Avgolemono is the Greek egg-and-lemon sauce, used to finish chicken soups and to sauce dolmas and vegetables. Egg yolks or whole eggs are tempered with hot broth and lemon so the liquid thickens without scrambling. Related egg-lemon sauces exist around the eastern Mediterranean. This is the sauce version for cooked vegetables or chicken.

Cultural background

The skill is tempering. A boil after the egg goes in will curdle it. The lemon should taste clear, not hidden by cream. There is no cream in the Greek sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Egg and lemon thickened into hot broth taste silky and tart. There is no cream. If the pot boils after the egg goes in, the egg scrambles and the sauce tastes grainy.

How it is made

Eggs and lemon are whisked, hot broth is added slowly, and the mixture returns to a gentle heat until it thickens.

Authentic touch

Temper the eggs with hot broth, then return the sauce to low heat and stop as soon as it thickens. Do not boil.

How it is used

Spoon it over stuffed grape leaves, chicken, or boiled vegetables.

Field notes

  • Avgolemono means egg-lemon. Oil is not the fat. The egg is.
  • If the sauce boils after the egg is in, it scrambles.

Ingredients

  • 2 eggs
  • 1/4 cup lemon juice
  • 2 cups hot chicken broth
  • Salt
  • White pepper

Steps

  1. Whisk the eggs and lemon.
  2. Whisk in the hot broth a ladle at a time.
  3. Return it to the pot over low heat and stir until it thickens slightly. Do not boil.
  4. Salt and pepper.
  5. Serve over dolmas or chicken.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • The soup version uses more broth.
  • A curdled pot can be blended briefly, and it will be frothier than a careful sauce.

Pairings

  • dolmas
  • chicken
  • artichokes
