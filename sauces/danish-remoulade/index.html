Danish Remoulade

Mayonnaise, curry, pickles, and cabbage: sweet, sharp, and chunky.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 8 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Danish remoulade is a mayonnaise sauce with finely chopped pickles, cabbage or cauliflower, and curry, served with fried fish, roast beef, and hot dogs. It is sweeter and more vegetable-heavy than French rémoulade. It became a standard Danish accompaniment in the twentieth century.

Cultural background

It is on the plate with fried plaice and on the red sausage. The texture is piccalilli-like inside a mayonnaise. French rémoulade is herbs and capers in a smoother sauce.

Why it tastes this way

Pickles, a little cabbage, and curry in mayonnaise taste sweet, sharp, and chunky. That is different from French rémoulade, which is herbs and capers in a smoother sauce. It cuts fried fish.

How it is made

Finely chopped pickled vegetables and curry are stirred into mayonnaise.

Authentic touch

Chop the pickle and cabbage very small. Large pieces make it a salad, not a sauce.

How it is used

Serve it with fried fish, roast beef, or a hot dog.

Field notes

  • Danish remoulade is closer to a curry pickle mayonnaise than to the French herb sauce of the same name.
  • It is everyday food with fish and with sausages.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup mayonnaise
  • 1/4 cup finely chopped pickled cucumber
  • 2 tablespoons finely chopped cauliflower or cabbage
  • 1 teaspoon curry powder
  • 1 teaspoon mustard
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 tablespoon pickle juice

Steps

  1. Stir all the ingredients together.
  2. Rest 20 minutes.
  3. Taste for sugar and curry.
  4. Refrigerate.
  5. Serve with fried fish or roast beef.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • French rémoulade is the herb and caper cousin.
  • A spoon of the piccalilli juices loosens this one.

Pairings

  • fried fish
  • roast beef
  • pølser
