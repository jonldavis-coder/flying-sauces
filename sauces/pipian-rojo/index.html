Pipián Rojo

Pumpkin seed and dried chile: nutty, savory, and warm.

20 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 6 · about 5 cups

History

Pipián is a Mexican sauce thickened with ground pumpkin seeds, red or green depending on the chiles and herbs. It is older in spirit than chocolate mole and is a different family. The seeds are the body. Chile seasons the red version.

Cultural background

It is served with chicken or pork. Toasting the seeds is what makes them taste round instead of raw and grassy. The sauce should be thick enough to coat.

Why it tastes this way

Ground pepitas, toasted and fried, taste nutty and thicken the sauce without flour. Dried red chile adds heat and color. Raw, untoasted seed tastes flat.

How it is made

Toasted pumpkin seeds and soaked chiles are blended, fried, and simmered with stock.

Authentic touch

Toast the seeds until they pop and smell nutty, grind them, and fry the puree. Do not skip the toast.

How it is used

Serve it with chicken and rice or tortillas.

Field notes

  • The thickener is seed, not a roux.
  • Green and red pipián are siblings with different chiles and herbs.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup pumpkin seeds
  • 4 dried guajillo chiles, soaked
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 2 cups chicken stock
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Toast the seeds until they pop. Grind them.
  2. Blend the seeds with the chiles, garlic, and 1 cup stock.
  3. Fry the puree in the oil 5 minutes.
  4. Add the rest of the stock and salt and simmer 20 minutes.
  5. Serve with chicken.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Pipián verde uses tomatillo and herbs.
  • A peanut mole is a different sauce.

Pairings

  • chicken
  • rice
  • tortillas
