Ocopa Sauce

Ají amarillo, peanut, and huacatay: nutty, herbal, and warm.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 4 · about 2 cups

  • Vegetarian

History

Ocopa is the Arequipa sauce served cold over boiled potatoes. Ají amarillo, peanuts or walnuts, and huacatay, a black-mint herb, are traditional. Crackers or bread sometimes thicken it. This version uses peanuts and notes huacatay as the herb to seek out.

Cultural background

It is a cold potato dish, not a hot stew. Huacatay tastes herbal and slightly resinous. Cilantro is not the same. If you cannot find it, the sauce will be incomplete, and this page says so.

Why it tastes this way

Ají amarillo is fruity and hot. Peanuts make it rich. Huacatay is the herbal note that makes ocopa taste like Arequipa rather than like a plain peanut sauce.

How it is made

Ají amarillo, peanut, garlic, and huacatay are blended with milk or evaporated milk into a cold sauce.

Authentic touch

Blend ají, toasted peanuts, and huacatay with a little milk or water until pourable, and serve it cold over warm potatoes.

How it is used

Spoon it over boiled potatoes.

Field notes

  • Ocopa is a cold sauce from Arequipa for potatoes.
  • Huacatay is the herb. It is not cilantro.

Ingredients

  • 1/4 cup ají amarillo paste
  • 1/2 cup roasted peanuts
  • 1 garlic clove
  • 1/4 cup huacatay leaves, or a smaller amount of jarred paste
  • 1/2 cup evaporated milk
  • 2 tablespoons oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Blend everything until smooth.
  2. Thin with a spoon of water if needed.
  3. Taste for salt.
  4. Serve cold over warm potatoes.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Walnuts replace peanuts in some houses.
  • Without huacatay, say the herb is missing rather than pretending cilantro matches.

Pairings

  • boiled potatoes
  • hard-boiled eggs
  • olives
