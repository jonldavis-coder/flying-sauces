Ranch Dressing

Buttermilk, mayonnaise, garlic, and dill: tangy, herbal, and cool.

10 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 8 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Ranch dressing was developed by Steve Henson in the 1950s, first served to workers in Alaska and then at Hidden Valley Ranch near Santa Barbara. Buttermilk, mayonnaise, garlic, and herbs are the taste. Dried herbs are part of the classic pantry version.

Cultural background

It became a salad dressing and a dip. Buttermilk is the tang. A rest lets the dried dill and garlic hydrate.

Why it tastes this way

Buttermilk is tangy and thin. Mayonnaise makes it rich. Garlic and dill are the savory-herbal notes. Without a rest, dried herbs taste dusty.

How it is made

Mayonnaise, buttermilk, garlic, and dried herbs are stirred and chilled.

Authentic touch

Stir it and refrigerate it for at least an hour before you serve it so the garlic and dill soften.

How it is used

Use it on lettuce or as a dip for vegetables.

Field notes

  • Steve Henson’s Hidden Valley recipe is the origin of the American dressing.
  • The chill time is part of the flavor.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup mayonnaise
  • 1/2 cup buttermilk
  • 1 garlic clove, minced
  • 1 teaspoon dried dill
  • 1/2 teaspoon onion powder
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice

Steps

  1. Stir everything together.
  2. Refrigerate at least 1 hour.
  3. Taste for salt.
  4. Thin with more buttermilk if you want a pourable dressing.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Sour cream instead of some mayonnaise makes it thicker.
  • Fresh dill is brighter than dried.

Pairings

  • lettuce
  • carrot sticks
  • wings
