Salsa Vizcaína

Dried choricero pepper and onion: sweet, deep, and smooth.

30 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 4 · about 2 cups

History

Salsa vizcaína is the Basque sauce of dried choricero peppers and onion, the sauce of bacalao a la vizcaína. The peppers are soaked and scraped. Onion is cooked slowly in olive oil. Tomato appears in some pots and is not the main flavor. The sauce is a Bizkaia classic, not a general Spanish tomato sauce.

Cultural background

Salt cod and this sauce are festive food, especially around holidays. The choricero pepper is the ingredient that makes it vizcaína. Paprika alone is only an approximation.

Why it tastes this way

Dried choricero pepper and slow onion, not a pile of fresh tomato, are the sweet, deep taste. The pepper pulp is scraped from soaked skins. Onion cooked until sweet is the other half.

How it is made

Onion cooks slowly in oil, choricero pulp is added, and the sauce is simmered and blended smooth.

Authentic touch

Scrape the pepper pulp and discard the skins. Cook the onion slowly until it is sweet before the pulp goes in.

How it is used

Serve it with desalted salt cod or other white fish.

Field notes

  • The pepper pulp, not a pile of fresh tomato, is the body of the sauce.
  • Bacalao a la vizcaína is the dish that carries it.

Ingredients

  • 6 dried choricero peppers, or 3 anchos as a stand-in
  • 1/3 cup olive oil
  • 2 onions, finely sliced
  • 1 garlic clove
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Soak the peppers until soft. Scrape out the pulp and discard the skins.
  2. Cook the onions in the oil very slowly until sweet, about 25 minutes.
  3. Add the pepper pulp and a splash of the soaking water.
  4. Simmer 10 minutes and blend until smooth.
  5. Salt and serve with fish.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of tomato is used by some cooks and argued over by others.
  • If choricero peppers are unavailable, the sauce will not taste the same. Ancho is only a distant stand-in.

Pairings

  • salt cod
  • potatoes
  • olive oil
