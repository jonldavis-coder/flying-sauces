General Tso Sauce

Soy, vinegar, sugar, and chile: sweet, sour, and hot.

5 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

General Tso’s chicken is named for the Qing general Zuo Zongtang. Chef Peng Chang-kuei developed a related chicken dish in Taiwan in the 1950s. The sweet, crispy, deep-fried version that became famous in the United States was adapted later for American Chinese restaurants. This is that sweet-hot sauce, not a Qing court recipe.

Cultural background

The chicken is fried and then tossed in the sauce. It is a restaurant dish of the Chinese diaspora in America. Hunan and Taiwanese versions are often less sweet.

Why it tastes this way

Soy, sugar, vinegar, and chile taste sweet, sour, and hot. The American Chinese version is sweeter than many earlier versions. The chicken is fried first so the sauce coats a crust.

How it is made

Soy, vinegar, sugar, stock, and chile simmer and are thickened, then tossed with fried chicken.

Authentic touch

Thicken the sauce and toss it with hot fried chicken. This page uses water, not a claim of a secret restaurant stock.

How it is used

Toss just-fried chicken in the sauce and serve with rice.

Field notes

  • The sweet crispy dish most Americans know was shaped in American Chinese restaurants.
  • Peng Chang-kuei’s earlier Taiwan version is part of the history and was not identical to the later sweet one.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup water
  • 2 tablespoons soy sauce
  • 2 tablespoons sugar
  • 1 tablespoon rice vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon doubanjiang or chili garlic paste
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch mixed with 1 tablespoon water
  • 1 garlic clove, minced

Steps

  1. Simmer the stock, soy, sugar, vinegar, chile paste, and garlic 3 minutes.
  2. Stir in the slurry until glossy.
  3. Toss with hot fried chicken.
  4. Serve at once with rice.
  5. The sauce should be sweet, sour, and hot.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A less sweet sauce is closer to some Taiwanese versions.
  • Orange sauce is a citrus cousin and is not given its own page.

Pairings

  • fried chicken
  • broccoli
  • rice
