Marie Rose Sauce

Mayonnaise, tomato, and a little Worcestershire: creamy, sweet, and savory.

5 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1 cup

History

Marie Rose sauce is the British prawn-cocktail sauce: mayonnaise, tomato ketchup, lemon, and often a dash of Worcestershire. Prawn cocktail became a British restaurant standard in the 1960s and 1970s. Stories about who named the sauce disagree, so this page does not pick an inventor. It is close to American cocktail sauce only in purpose. The British one is mayonnaise-based.

Cultural background

It is starter food, on lettuce with cold prawns, and a sandwich spread. The color is pink from the ketchup.

Why it tastes this way

Mayonnaise and ketchup taste creamy and slightly sweet. A dash of Worcestershire adds savor from anchovy. It is the British prawn-cocktail sauce, not the American ketchup-and-horseradish cocktail sauce.

How it is made

Mayonnaise and ketchup are stirred with lemon and a small dash of Worcestershire.

Authentic touch

Keep the ketchup to a spoon or two. Too much and it tastes like ketchup. Chill it.

How it is used

Spoon it over cold prawns or use it in a prawn sandwich.

Field notes

  • British prawn cocktail sauce is a mayonnaise sauce. American cocktail sauce is ketchup and horseradish.
  • The naming stories conflict, so none is treated as fact here.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup mayonnaise
  • 2 tablespoons ketchup
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice
  • 1/2 teaspoon Worcestershire sauce
  • Pinch of black pepper

Steps

  1. Stir all the ingredients together.
  2. Taste for lemon.
  3. Chill.
  4. Spoon over cold prawns.
  5. Use within a few days.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A few drops of brandy show up in restaurant versions.
  • American cocktail sauce skips the mayonnaise and uses horseradish.

Pairings

  • cold prawns
  • lettuce
  • brown bread
