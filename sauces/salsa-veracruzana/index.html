Salsa Veracruzana

Tomato, olive, caper, and bay: savory, briny, and mild.

15 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 4 · about 3 cups

History

Salsa veracruzana is the tomato sauce of Veracruz for fish, with olive, caper, and often bay and thyme. Those Mediterranean pantry items arrived with Spanish colonial trade and stayed in the Gulf kitchen. The sauce is cooked and spooned over fish.

Cultural background

Pescado a la veracruzana is the dish. The olives and capers are the briny note that plain tomato sauce does not have. Jalapeño can heat it. It does not have to.

Why it tastes this way

Tomato is the body. Olives and capers, preserved in salt and brine, taste savory because Veracruz cooking kept those Spanish stores. Herbs and a short simmer round the tomato.

How it is made

Onion, garlic, and tomato simmer, then olive, caper, and herbs finish the sauce.

Authentic touch

Add olives and capers near the end so they stay distinct. Do not blend them smooth.

How it is used

Spoon it over baked or simmered white fish.

Field notes

  • Olives and capers mark the Gulf sauce’s Spanish pantry.
  • It is a fish sauce, cooked, not a raw salsa.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 onion, chopped
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 4 tomatoes, chopped
  • 1/4 cup green olives, sliced
  • 1 tablespoon capers
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Cook the onion and garlic in the oil.
  2. Add the tomatoes, bay, and salt and simmer 15 minutes.
  3. Stir in the olives and capers.
  4. Simmer 5 minutes.
  5. Serve over fish.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A pickled jalapeño makes it hotter.
  • Without olive and caper it is no longer veracruzana.

Pairings

  • snapper
  • rice
  • olives
