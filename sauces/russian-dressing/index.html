Russian Dressing

Mayonnaise, chili sauce, and a sharp note: creamy and spicy.

5 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 1 cup

History

Russian dressing is an American mayonnaise dressing with chili sauce or ketchup and often horseradish or relish, named Russian without being a Russian recipe. It was a standard by the early twentieth century and is a usual spread for a Reuben sandwich. It is sharper than Thousand Island.

Cultural background

The Reuben uses it on corned beef, sauerkraut, and cheese. Horseradish or hot sauce is the sharp edge. Relish, if any, is smaller than in Thousand Island.

Why it tastes this way

Mayonnaise is rich. Chili sauce is sweet and tangy. Horseradish adds a sharp heat that Thousand Island usually lacks. That sharpness stands up to corned beef.

How it is made

Mayonnaise, ketchup or chili sauce, and horseradish are stirred together.

Authentic touch

Add horseradish or a hot note. If it tastes only sweet, it has drifted into Thousand Island.

How it is used

Spread it on a Reuben or dress a wedge.

Field notes

  • The name is American. The dressing is not a Russian national sauce.
  • It is the usual Reuben spread.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup mayonnaise
  • 3 tablespoons ketchup
  • 1 tablespoon prepared horseradish
  • 1 teaspoon vinegar
  • 1/4 teaspoon paprika
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Stir everything.
  2. Chill 15 minutes.
  3. Taste for horseradish.
  4. Spread it on sandwiches.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Thousand Island uses more relish and less horseradish.
  • A chopped egg is an older addition.

Pairings

  • Reuben sandwich
  • wedge salad
