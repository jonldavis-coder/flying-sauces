Kecap Manis

Sweet soy, garlic, and star anise: thick, salty, and sweet.

5 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 12 · about 1 cup

History

Kecap manis is Indonesian sweet soy sauce, a pillar of Javanese cooking. Commercial kecap is fermented soy sweetened with palm sugar. This recipe is the home reduction of soy sauce with palm sugar and spices. It is a kitchen version of the flavor, not a from-scratch soy fermentation.

Cultural background

It glazes satay, nasi goreng, and mie goreng. The thickness is what lets it coat noodles. Salty soy alone will not do that job.

Why it tastes this way

Palm sugar cooked into soy tastes thick and sweet-salty. Reduction is why it coats noodles. This home pot starts from soy sauce. It is not a from-scratch ferment.

How it is made

Soy sauce, palm sugar, garlic, and a spice simmer until syrupy.

Authentic touch

Simmer until syrupy and cool it. It thickens as it cools. Use it as a glaze, not by the cup.

How it is used

Brush it on satay or toss it with fried rice and noodles.

Field notes

  • Manis means sweet. Kecap asin is the salty soy.
  • A home reduction starts from soy sauce. It does not replace the ferment.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup soy sauce
  • 1/2 cup palm or brown sugar
  • 2 garlic cloves, smashed
  • 1 star anise
  • 1/4 cup water

Steps

  1. Simmer all the ingredients until syrupy, about 10 minutes.
  2. Remove the garlic and star anise.
  3. Cool. It will thicken.
  4. Use it to glaze satay or season fried rice.
  5. Refrigerate.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • The fermented commercial sauce is the pantry original. This is a reduction.
  • Star anise is a common spice in home reductions and not in every bottle.

Pairings

  • satay
  • nasi goreng
  • mie goreng
