Chip Shop Curry Sauce

History

British chip-shop curry sauce is a mild, thick spiced gravy sold on chips, especially in the north of England and in Scotland. It grew up beside fish and chips in the twentieth century, separate from Indian restaurant curries and from Chinese takeaway sauces. It is a chip-shop sauce, not a curry paste.

Cultural background

It is ladled over chips or into a pie. The spice level is gentle on purpose. Onion and curry powder are the flavor, not a long list of whole spices.

Why it tastes this way

Mild curry powder, onion, and a light thickener taste savory and slightly sweet, not hot. It was made to go on fried potatoes, so it is a gravy, not a long meat curry.

How it is made

Onion softens, curry powder is fried briefly, and stock and a little flour or cornflour make a smooth gravy.

Authentic touch

Fry the curry powder in the onion fat for a minute so it does not taste of raw powder. Keep it pourable.

How it is used

Pour it over chips, or serve it with a pie.

Field notes

  • Chip-shop curry sauce is its own British category, milder than most restaurant curries.
  • It is a gravy for fried potatoes, not a simmered meat curry.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 small onion, minced
  • 1 tablespoon curry powder
  • 1 tablespoon flour
  • 2 cups vegetable stock
  • 1 teaspoon tomato paste
  • 1/2 teaspoon sugar
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Cook the onion in the oil until soft.
  2. Stir in the curry powder and flour for 1 minute.
  3. Add the stock and tomato paste and simmer 10 minutes.
  4. Stir in the sugar and salt.
  5. Blend if you want it as smooth as a chip shop’s.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A teaspoon of mango chutney makes it sweeter, which some shops do.
  • Raising the cayenne makes it less like a chip-shop sauce.

Pairings

  • chips
  • meat pie
  • fried fish
