Cuban Enchilado Sauce

Tomato, cumin, garlic, and wine, with shrimp: savory, brick-red, and mild.

15 min prep · 20 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 cups with the shrimp

History

Camarones enchilados is a Cuban shrimp dish in a tomato sauce seasoned with garlic, cumin, and often white wine or vinegar. The word here means the shrimp are cooked in that seasoned sauce. It is not the Mexican enchilada and it does not use a dried-chile purée or a tortilla. The sauce is the tomato-cumin pot. The shrimp are what the pot is for.

Cultural background

It is weeknight and restaurant food, served with white rice so the sauce has somewhere to go. Green pepper and onion are the same sofrito pair that starts many Cuban pots.

Why it tastes this way

Tomato is sweet and acid. Cumin and oregano make it savory rather than like a plain marinara. Wine, reduced, adds a sharp fruit note. The shrimp taste briny and sweet once they cook in that sauce, which is why the pot tastes of the sea and not only of tomato. Chile is not the heat. A spicy Mexican enchilada sauce is a different food.

How it is made

A sofrito of onion, pepper, and garlic takes tomato, cumin, and wine. Shrimp warm through in the finished sauce.

Authentic touch

Cook the vegetables until they are sweet before the tomato goes in. Add the shrimp at the end so they stay tender. Do not wrap anything in a tortilla and still call it this dish.

How it is used

Serve with white rice. The sauce should be loose enough to spoon.

Field notes

  • Enchilado in Cuba is a shrimp and tomato sauce, not a Mexican enchilada.
  • Cumin, not a dried chile paste, is the spice that marks the pot.

Ingredients

  • 1 pound peeled shrimp
  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 1/2 cups crushed tomato
  • 1/3 cup dry white wine
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Salt the shrimp and set them aside.
  2. Cook the onion and pepper in the oil until soft.
  3. Add the garlic, cumin, and oregano and cook 1 minute.
  4. Add the tomato and wine and simmer 10 minutes.
  5. Stir in the shrimp and cook just until they turn pink. Serve with rice.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A splash of vinegar can replace the wine.
  • Some cooks add a little ketchup for sweetness. Crushed tomato is enough.

Pairings

  • shrimp
  • white rice
