Italian Salsa Verde

Parsley, capers, anchovy, and olive oil: sharp, herbal, and salty.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 1 cup

History

Italian salsa verde is a chopped parsley sauce with capers, anchovy, garlic, and olive oil, served with bollito misto and grilled meats. It is not Mexican tomatillo salsa verde and it is not chimichurri, though all three are green herb sauces. The Italian one is defined by capers and anchovy.

Cultural background

In Piedmont and Lombardy it cuts the richness of boiled meats. A spoon of bread crumb is traditional in some pots and is omitted here so the sauce stays gluten-free.

Why it tastes this way

Parsley, capers, and anchovy in olive oil taste salty and herbal. It is chopped, not cooked, so it stays bright against boiled meat. It is not a tomatillo salsa and not chimichurri.

How it is made

Parsley, capers, anchovy, and garlic are chopped fine and stirred with olive oil and vinegar.

Authentic touch

Chop the anchovy and capers into the parsley so they disappear into the sauce. A whole anchovy on top is not the texture.

How it is used

Spoon it over boiled meats, grilled fish, or warm potatoes.

Field notes

  • Mexican salsa verde is tomatillo. This one is parsley.
  • Anchovy is traditional. Leaving it out makes a different green sauce.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup finely chopped parsley
  • 2 tablespoons capers
  • 2 anchovy fillets, minced
  • 1 small garlic clove, minced
  • 1 tablespoon red wine vinegar
  • 1/3 cup olive oil
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Chop the parsley, capers, anchovy, and garlic together.
  2. Stir in the vinegar and oil.
  3. Rest 10 minutes.
  4. Add salt only if needed.
  5. Serve with boiled or grilled meat.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Bagnet verd is the Piedmontese name for a close sauce.
  • A mustardy version leans toward sauce gribiche.

Pairings

  • bollito misto
  • grilled fish
  • potatoes
