Puerto Rican Sofrito

This stop is on Caribbean by boat, traveled by boat.

Next stop: Trinidadian Pepper Sauce · Trinidad and Tobago · Caribbean by boat

Ají dulce, pepper, cilantro, and culantro: sweet, herbal, and savory.

20 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 10 · about 2 cups

History

Puerto Rican sofrito is a blended base of onion, garlic, sweet pepper, ají dulce, cilantro, and culantro, often with tomato and oil. It starts beans, rice, and stews. Spanish sofrito is a cooked tomato-onion base and is not the same blend. Ají dulce is aromatic and not very hot.

Cultural background

A freezer jar of sofrito is how many kitchens start dinner. Culantro, recao, tastes stronger than cilantro. Both appear in many blends. Frying the raw blend is the step that makes the pot taste right.

Why it tastes this way

Sweet peppers and ají dulce taste fragrant rather than hot. Culantro and cilantro are herbal. Fried in oil with tomato, the blend tastes savory and sweet, which is the base under rice and beans.

How it is made

Peppers, onion, garlic, and herbs are blended, then fried as the start of a dish.

Authentic touch

Blend it raw, then fry a few spoonfuls in oil until the raw smell is gone before you add rice or beans. If you cannot find ají dulce, a bit of bell pepper is milder and not the same.

How it is used

Use it as the base of rice, beans, and stews.

Field notes

  • Ají dulce is fragrant, not a habanero’s heat.
  • Puerto Rican sofrito and Spanish sofrito share a name and not a recipe.

Ingredients

  • 1 green bell pepper
  • 1 onion
  • 6 garlic cloves
  • 1 cup cilantro
  • 1/2 cup culantro if available, or more cilantro
  • 1 tomato
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Blend the vegetables, herbs, and salt.
  2. Store cold.
  3. Fry a portion in oil until fragrant before building a rice or bean pot.
  4. Use the jar within a few days.
  5. Taste the finished dish for salt.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Cuban sofrito leans on more tomato and different peppers.
  • A spoon of tomato sauce in the fry is common.

Pairings

  • arroz con gandules
  • habichuelas
  • stews
