Molho Campeiro

Onion, tomato, vinegar, and bell pepper: fresh and sharp.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 8 · about 3 cups

History

Molho campeiro, also called vinagrete, is the Brazilian chopped sauce of onion, tomato, pepper, and vinegar served with grilled meat and feijoada. It is a fresh relish. The vinegar is the dressing.

Cultural background

Churrasco plates expect it. The onion is often the loudest piece. A rest in vinegar softens it.

Why it tastes this way

Vinegar and raw onion taste sharp against fatty grilled meat. Tomato and pepper add juice and sweetness. Oil is optional. Nothing is cooked, so it stays fresh.

How it is made

Onion, tomato, and pepper are diced and dressed with vinegar and salt.

Authentic touch

Dice everything small, salt it, and let it sit in vinegar for at least 15 minutes.

How it is used

Serve it with grilled beef or feijoada.

Field notes

  • Vinagrete is the other name, and vinegar is the reason.
  • It is raw, like a salsa criolla.

Ingredients

  • 1 onion, finely diced
  • 2 tomatoes, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 1/3 cup vinegar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Toss everything.
  2. Rest 20 minutes.
  3. Taste for vinegar.
  4. Serve with grilled meat.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A hotter version adds malagueta.
  • Cilantro is used in some houses.

Pairings

  • churrasco
  • feijoada
  • farofa
