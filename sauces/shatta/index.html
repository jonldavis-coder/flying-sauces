Shatta

Chile and salt, with lemon or vinegar: hot, fresh, and sharp.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 12 · about 1 cup

History

Shatta is the fresh chile condiment of Egypt and the Levant. Chiles are chopped with salt and sometimes lemon, garlic, or a little oil. Some households ferment it. This recipe is the fresh salted version, kept cold, not a long ferment.

Cultural background

It is on the breakfast table with eggs, ful, and bread, and next to grilled meat. Green or red follows the chile. Heat is the point.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh chile and salt taste hot and bright because the relish is not cooked into a ketchup. Lemon sharpens it. It sits next to mild beans and eggs.

How it is made

Chiles are chopped with salt, garlic, and lemon and left to soften. They are not cooked.

Authentic touch

Chop it and salt it, then wait. This fresh version is not a ferment. Keep it cold.

How it is used

Spoon it beside eggs, ful, or grilled meat.

Field notes

  • Shatta is a chile relish, not a cooked ketchup-style hot sauce.
  • This page gives the fresh version and does not pretend it is a long ferment.

Ingredients

  • 8 fresh green or red chiles, finely chopped
  • 2 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil

Steps

  1. Stir the chiles, garlic, and salt.
  2. Add the lemon and oil.
  3. Rest 20 minutes.
  4. Taste. It should be very hot.
  5. Refrigerate.

Refrigerate and use within a week. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A fermented shatta is saltier and softer and takes days.
  • Green chiles make the usual bright version.

Pairings

  • ful medames
  • eggs
  • grilled meat
