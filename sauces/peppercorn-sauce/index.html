Sauce au Poivre

Cracked pepper, brandy, stock, and cream: hot, savory, and rich.

5 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 4 · about 3/4 cup

History

Steak au poivre and its sauce became a French bistro standard in the twentieth century. Cracked peppercorns are pressed into the meat, and the pan is deglazed with brandy and stock, then finished with cream. The pepper is black pepper, not chile, so the heat on this page is marked mild.

Cultural background

It is a restaurant pan sauce, made after the steak is seared. The cream is the usual bistro finish. A cream-free reduction also exists.

Why it tastes this way

Cracked black pepper, brandy, and cream are the bistro taste. The pepper is toasted in the pan, so it is fragrant rather than raw. Cream rounds the heat. This heat is pepper, not chile.

How it is made

Pepper and the browned pan are deglazed with brandy and stock, reduced, and finished with cream.

Authentic touch

Crack the pepper coarsely and let it fry in the steak drippings for a moment before you deglaze. Powdered pepper will not taste the same.

How it is used

Pour it over seared steaks. Press extra pepper onto the meat before cooking if you want the full dish.

Field notes

  • The heat is black pepper, which this guide does not count as chili heat.
  • The sauce is a pan sauce. It is not a simmered gravy started from scratch.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon cracked black pepper
  • 2 tablespoons brandy or cognac
  • 1/2 cup beef stock
  • 1/3 cup heavy cream
  • 1 tablespoon butter
  • Salt

Steps

  1. After searing steaks, leave the pepper and browned bits in the pan.
  2. Add the brandy off the flame if it might flare, then simmer 30 seconds.
  3. Add the stock and reduce by half.
  4. Stir in the cream and butter and simmer until the sauce coats a spoon.
  5. Salt lightly and pour over the steaks.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Green peppercorns in brine are the softer, sharper variation.
  • Skip the cream for a darker reduction.

Pairings

  • steak
  • frites
  • green beans
