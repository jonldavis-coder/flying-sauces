Sauce Andalouse

Mayonnaise, tomato, and pepper: creamy, slightly sweet, and mild.

10 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 1 cup

History

Sauce andalouse is a Belgian mayonnaise sauce with tomato and roasted or raw pepper, a standard frites sauce alongside mayonnaise. The name points at Andalusia and does not make it a Spanish recipe. It is a twentieth-century Belgian snack-bar sauce.

Cultural background

Friteries offer it next to mayonnaise, samurai sauce, and others. It is pink-orange and milder than a hot sauce. The pepper is the difference from plain tomato mayonnaise.

Why it tastes this way

Mayonnaise, tomato, and pepper taste creamy and slightly sweet. The pepper is what separates it from plain tomato mayonnaise. It is a Belgian fry-shop sauce despite the Spanish name.

How it is made

Mayonnaise is stirred with tomato paste or ketchup and finely chopped pepper.

Authentic touch

Mince the pepper fine and let the sauce rest so the pepper softens into the mayonnaise.

How it is used

Serve it with fries or on a sandwich.

Field notes

  • The Andalusian name is Belgian usage, not a Seville recipe.
  • It is one of the standard sauces at a Belgian friterie.

Ingredients

  • 3/4 cup mayonnaise
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
  • 2 tablespoons finely minced red bell pepper
  • 1/2 teaspoon vinegar
  • Pinch of cayenne
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Stir all the ingredients together.
  2. Rest 15 minutes so the pepper softens.
  3. Taste for salt.
  4. Refrigerate.
  5. Serve with fries.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A roasted pepper makes it sweeter.
  • Ketchup instead of tomato paste makes it closer to a burger sauce.

Pairings

  • frites
  • fritkot sandwiches
  • sausages
