Haydari

Strained yogurt, garlic, dill, and mint: thick, herbal, and tangy.

10 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 6 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Haydari is a Turkish strained-yogurt meze, thicker than cacık, with garlic and herbs such as dill and mint. It is spreadable rather than soupy. Feta is sometimes mashed in. It is a meze plate sauce, not a cucumber soup.

Cultural background

It is eaten with bread at the start of a meal, next to grilled vegetables. The yogurt should be strained or the haydari will not hold a shape.

Why it tastes this way

Strained yogurt, garlic, and herbs taste thick and tangy because the whey is gone. It spreads. Cacık, with more water and cucumber, is the looser relative.

How it is made

Strained yogurt is beaten with garlic, dried mint, fresh dill, and a little olive oil.

Authentic touch

Start with strained yogurt. If it is thin, strain it. Haydari should hold a shape on the plate.

How it is used

Spread it on a plate, finish with oil, and eat it with bread.

Field notes

  • Haydari is thick enough to spread. Cacık is often thin enough to sip.
  • Straining the yogurt matters more than any one herb.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups strained yogurt
  • 1 garlic clove, minced
  • 1 tablespoon chopped dill
  • 1 teaspoon dried mint
  • 1 tablespoon olive oil
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Beat the yogurt with the garlic, dill, mint, and salt.
  2. Spread it on a plate.
  3. Finish with the olive oil.
  4. Rest 10 minutes.
  5. Serve with bread.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A spoon of crumbled feta makes it saltier.
  • Cacık is the looser cucumber yogurt and a different dish.

Pairings

  • warm bread
  • grilled peppers
  • kebabs
