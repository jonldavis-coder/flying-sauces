Bumbu Kacang

Peanut, sweet soy, and chile: rich, savory, and lightly hot.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 6 · about 2 cups

History

Bumbu kacang is the Indonesian peanut sauce for satay, gado-gado, and ketoprak. Fried shallot, garlic, chile, and sweet soy season the ground peanuts. It is related to other satay sauces and is spicier and more savory than many Thai coconut-peanut sauces, which are noted here as a variation rather than a second recipe.

Cultural background

Street satay is unfinished without it. The sauce should be pourable, not a stiff peanut butter.

Why it tastes this way

Peanuts taste rich, kecap manis tastes sweet-salty, and chile heats the sauce. Water turns peanut butter into a pourable satay sauce. Coconut milk would push it toward a different, Thai-style sauce.

How it is made

Shallot, garlic, and chile fry, then peanut butter, kecap manis, and water simmer into a sauce.

Authentic touch

Simmer peanut butter with kecap manis and water until smooth. Thin it until it pours.

How it is used

Spoon it over grilled satay or cooked vegetables.

Field notes

  • The same peanut sauce serves both grilled meat and vegetable salads in Indonesia.
  • Kecap manis is the sweetness. Plain soy will not taste the same.

Ingredients

  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 2 shallots, sliced
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 red chile
  • 1/2 cup peanut butter
  • 2 tablespoons kecap manis or sweet soy
  • 3/4 cup water
  • 1 tablespoon lime juice
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Fry the shallot, garlic, and chile in the oil.
  2. Add the peanut butter, kecap manis, and water and simmer until smooth.
  3. Stir in the lime and salt.
  4. Thin with water if it is pasty.
  5. Serve with satay.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A Thai satay sauce often adds coconut milk and is sweeter.
  • More chile is a household choice.

Pairings

  • chicken satay
  • gado-gado
  • rice cakes
