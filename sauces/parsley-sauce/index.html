Parsley Sauce

Milk, butter, and a heavy handful of parsley: mild, green, and saucy.

10 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 6 · about 2 cups

History

Parsley sauce is a white sauce finished with a large amount of chopped parsley. In Ireland it is the usual sauce for boiled bacon, the brined pork joint served with cabbage and potatoes. The same sauce is cooked in Britain with ham or fish. This page puts it with the Irish bacon plate, and it does not claim Ireland invented the white sauce.

Cultural background

The bacon is simmered, not roasted, so the table needs a hot sauce. Parsley is the herb that is cheap and green when the joint is pale. A thin pour is the point. A stiff paste will not coat the slices.

Why it tastes this way

Flour and butter taste of toasted milk fat once the roux cooks, and the milk keeps the sauce mild enough for salted pork. Parsley is added off the heat, so it stays green and grassy instead of turning brown and dull. Salt from the bacon is why the sauce itself is only lightly salted.

How it is made

A pale roux and milk make a thin white sauce. Parsley goes in at the end.

Authentic touch

Use a lot of parsley, not a garnish sprinkle. Stir it in after the sauce has thickened, away from a hard boil, so the color stays bright.

How it is used

Pour it over sliced boiled bacon, ham, or poached fish, with cabbage and potatoes.

Field notes

  • Boiled bacon, not corned beef, is the older Irish joint for this sauce.
  • The parsley is the flavor. A white sauce without it is a different sauce.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons butter
  • 2 tablespoons flour
  • 2 cups whole milk
  • 1 cup finely chopped parsley
  • Salt and white pepper
  • Pinch of nutmeg

Steps

  1. Melt the butter and stir in the flour. Cook 1 minute without browning.
  2. Whisk in the milk a little at a time.
  3. Simmer until the sauce coats a spoon, about 6 minutes.
  4. Season lightly with salt, pepper, and nutmeg.
  5. Take it off the heat and stir in the parsley.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A bay leaf in the milk is common and is removed before serving.
  • Britain uses the same sauce. The Irish plate is bacon and cabbage.

Pairings

  • boiled bacon
  • cabbage
  • potatoes
  • poached fish
