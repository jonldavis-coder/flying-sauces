Char Siu Sauce

Hoisin, soy, honey, and five-spice: sweet, savory, and glossy.

10 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 4 · about 3/4 cup

History

Char siu is Cantonese roasted pork, lacquered with a sweet soy marinade of hoisin or fermented bean sauce, sugar or honey, and five-spice. The red color of shop char siu often comes from fermented red bean curd or food coloring. This marinade uses hoisin and honey and does not rely on dye.

Cultural background

The pork is roasted and the marinade becomes the glaze. It is eaten with rice or in noodle soups. The sugar burns if it meets high heat too early.

Why it tastes this way

Hoisin, soy, honey, and five-spice taste sweet and savory. The sugar lacquers the pork. Shop-window red is often color, not the honey. High heat too early burns the sugar.

How it is made

Hoisin, soy, honey, and five-spice coat pork, which is roasted and basted.

Authentic touch

Roast first and baste near the end. Boil any marinade that touched raw pork before you use it as a glaze.

How it is used

Slice the roasted pork and serve it with rice. Boil any marinade that touched raw meat before basting at the end.

Field notes

  • Char siu means fork roast, from the way the meat was hung.
  • The red of many shop windows is color, not the honey.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons hoisin sauce
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1 tablespoon honey
  • 1 teaspoon five-spice powder
  • 1 teaspoon rice wine
  • 1 garlic clove, minced

Steps

  1. Stir all the ingredients.
  2. Coat pork shoulder or loin and refrigerate at least 2 hours.
  3. Roast until cooked through, basting near the end.
  4. Rest the meat before slicing.
  5. Serve with rice.

Boil marinade that has touched raw pork before you brush it on cooked meat.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Red fermented bean curd is the traditional color and savor in many shop recipes.
  • Honey can be swapped for maltose, which is stickier.

Pairings

  • steamed rice
  • noodles
  • scallions
