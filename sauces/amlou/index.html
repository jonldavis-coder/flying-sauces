Amlou

Almond, argan oil, and honey: toasted, rich, and sweet.

15 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 8 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Amlou is the almond, argan oil, and honey spread of southwestern Morocco, especially Amazigh cooking in the Souss. Argan oil is pressed from the kernels of the argan tree. Culinary argan oil, not a cosmetic oil, is what belongs in the bowl. It is eaten with bread at breakfast.

Cultural background

It is a breakfast dip more than a dinner sauce. The almonds are toasted. The honey and oil are stirred in while the nuts are still warm.

Why it tastes this way

Toasted almond, culinary argan oil, and honey taste of the Souss because argan oil is local and the almonds are toasted. A neutral oil will not taste like amlou.

How it is made

Toasted almonds are ground, then culinary argan oil and honey are worked in until the paste is loose enough to dip bread into.

Authentic touch

Use culinary argan oil, not a cosmetic oil. Stir it into warm ground almonds.

How it is used

Serve it with bread for breakfast.

Field notes

  • Culinary argan oil is the ingredient. Cosmetic oil is not for this bowl.
  • Amlou is a breakfast sauce of the Souss, not a general Moroccan dessert.

Ingredients

  • 1 1/2 cups blanched almonds
  • 1/4 cup culinary argan oil
  • 2 tablespoons honey
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Toast the almonds until golden and cool them slightly.
  2. Grind them to a paste.
  3. Stir in the argan oil, honey, and salt.
  4. Add a little more oil if it is too stiff to spread.
  5. Serve with bread.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • More oil makes it saucier. More almond makes it a paste.
  • A neutral oil is not a substitute for the argan flavor.

Pairings

  • bread
  • mint tea
