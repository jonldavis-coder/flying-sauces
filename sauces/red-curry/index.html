Thai Red Curry

The next field stop is a nearby sauce on the same map.

Next stop: Sriracha · Thailand

Dried red chile and coconut milk: hot, fragrant, and rich.

25 min prep · 35 min cook · Serves 4 · about 4 cups

History

Kaeng phet, Thai red curry, is built on a paste of dried red chiles, shallot, garlic, galangal, lemongrass, and shrimp paste. It is a central Thai curry, often with chicken, beef, or duck and with bamboo or Thai eggplant. The dried chile makes it redder and deeper than green curry.

Cultural background

It is weeknight and restaurant food, eaten with rice. The paste must be fried before the coconut milk is fully added, or the sauce tastes raw.

Why it tastes this way

Dried red chiles taste deeper and slightly smokier than fresh green ones. Coconut milk softens the heat. The paste still has to be fried or the sauce tastes raw.

How it is made

Soaked dried chiles are pounded with aromatics, fried in coconut cream, and simmered with meat and the rest of the coconut milk.

Authentic touch

Soak the dried chiles, pound the paste, and fry it in coconut cream before you add the meat.

How it is used

Serve it with rice.

Field notes

  • Red curry’s color comes from dried chiles, not from tomato.
  • Frying the paste is the step that wakes the spices.

Ingredients

  • 6 dried red chiles, soaked
  • 2 shallots
  • 3 garlic cloves
  • 1 tablespoon lemongrass
  • 1 teaspoon galangal
  • 1/2 teaspoon shrimp paste
  • 1 can (14 ounces) coconut milk
  • 1 tablespoon fish sauce
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 pound chicken thighs

Steps

  1. Drain the chiles and pound them with the shallot, garlic, lemongrass, galangal, and shrimp paste.
  2. Fry the paste in a little coconut cream until fragrant.
  3. Add the chicken and the remaining coconut milk, fish sauce, and sugar.
  4. Simmer until the chicken is cooked.
  5. Serve with rice.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Panang is thicker, sweeter, and often includes peanuts.
  • Green curry uses fresh green chiles instead.

Pairings

  • jasmine rice
  • Thai eggplant
  • chicken
