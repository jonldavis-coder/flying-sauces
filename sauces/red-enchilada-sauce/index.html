Red Enchilada Sauce

The next field stop is a nearby sauce on the same map.

Dried guajillo and ancho, smoothed into a sauce: mild, earthy, and warm.

20 min prep · 25 min cook · Serves 6 · about 3 cups

History

Red enchilada sauce is a smooth sauce of dried chiles, often guajillo and ancho, briefly fried and thinned with stock. It is built to soak a tortilla. It is smoother and milder than a chunky taquería salsa. American chili gravy with cumin and flour is a related Tex-Mex cousin.

Cultural background

The tortillas are dipped in the warm sauce before they are filled. The chile, not tomato, should be the main flavor. Toasting the chiles wakes them. Burning them makes the sauce bitter.

Why it tastes this way

Guajillo tastes bright and a little fruity. Ancho tastes raisiny. Frying the blended chile in fat rounds the raw bitterness. Stock makes it thin enough to coat a tortilla.

How it is made

Dried chiles are toasted, soaked, blended, fried, and thinned.

Authentic touch

Toast the dried chiles just until fragrant, soak, blend, and fry the puree. Strain if you want it silky.

How it is used

Dip tortillas in the warm sauce, fill them, and bake or serve them soft.

Field notes

  • The sauce should taste of chile, not of a can of tomato.
  • A bitter sauce usually means the chiles were burnt in the dry pan.

Ingredients

  • 4 guajillo chiles
  • 2 ancho chiles
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 2 cups water or vegetable stock
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1/2 teaspoon oregano

Steps

  1. Toast the chiles, stem and seed them, and soak in hot water 15 minutes.
  2. Blend with garlic, oregano, and 1 cup of the water.
  3. Fry the puree in the oil 5 minutes.
  4. Add the rest of the water and salt and simmer 10 minutes.
  5. Strain if you want it smooth.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A flour-thickened chili gravy is the Tex-Mex cousin.
  • New Mexico red chile sauce is a close regional relative.

Pairings

  • corn tortillas
  • cheese
  • chicken
