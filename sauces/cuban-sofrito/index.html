Cuban Sofrito

Onion, green pepper, garlic, and cumin, fried in oil: sweet and savory.

10 min prep · 15 min cook · Serves 8 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Cuban sofrito is a fried base of onion, green bell pepper, and garlic, often with cumin, oregano, and a little tomato. It seasons black beans, stews, and rice. It is not Puerto Rican sofrito. The Puerto Rican one is built on ají dulce and culantro and is often left uncooked as a paste. Those ingredients are not the Cuban pot. Spanish sofrito, with its own onion and tomato path, is a third kitchen.

Cultural background

The sofrito is cooked at the start of a bean pot or a stew so the oil takes up the vegetables. It is a cooking sauce, not usually a cold table relish. That job belongs to mojo or to salsa criolla.

Why it tastes this way

Frying onion and green pepper in oil makes them sweet. Garlic and cumin perfume that oil, which is why the whole pot tastes seasoned after one spoon of sofrito. Tomato, when it is used, adds acid. A raw paste of the same vegetables tastes sharp and grassy instead of sweet.

How it is made

Onion, pepper, and garlic fry in olive oil with cumin and oregano, then tomato cooks down.

Authentic touch

Cook it until the vegetables are soft and the oil looks orange from the tomato and pepper. Do not add culantro or ají dulce and still call it the Cuban version.

How it is used

Start black beans, stews, or rice with a spoonful. It is the seasoning, not the finished dish.

Field notes

  • Cuban sofrito is cooked. Puerto Rican sofrito is a different recipe, often raw.
  • Culantro is not the herb that defines this version.

Ingredients

  • 3 tablespoons olive oil
  • 1 onion, diced
  • 1 green bell pepper, diced
  • 4 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 teaspoon ground cumin
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1 cup crushed tomato
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Warm the oil and cook the onion and pepper until soft.
  2. Add the garlic, cumin, and oregano and cook 1 minute.
  3. Stir in the tomato and a pinch of salt.
  4. Simmer until the mixture is thick and the oil looks tinted, about 8 minutes.
  5. Use it to start beans, rice, or a stew.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Some cooks skip the tomato and use only the vegetables and spices.
  • A bay leaf can simmer in the beans after the sofrito goes in.

Pairings

  • black beans
  • rice
  • stews
  • shredded beef
