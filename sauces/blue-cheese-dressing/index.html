Blue Cheese Dressing

Blue cheese, buttermilk, and mayonnaise: salty, tangy, and rich.

10 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 8 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Blue cheese dressing is a mayonnaise and buttermilk dressing with crumbled blue cheese, the usual partner for Buffalo wings and for wedge salads in American restaurants. The cheese should stay in crumbles. A fully smooth dressing tastes flatter.

Cultural background

It cools spicy wings. The salt is in the cheese, so the dressing needs less added salt. A short rest softens the crumbles.

Why it tastes this way

Blue cheese is salty and sharp from its mold ripening. Buttermilk thins it and adds tang. Mayonnaise binds it. Crumbles are the texture people expect with wings.

How it is made

Blue cheese is mashed into mayonnaise and buttermilk.

Authentic touch

Mash some of the cheese into the base and leave the rest in chunks. Chill it.

How it is used

Serve it with wings or a lettuce wedge.

Field notes

  • Leave crumbles. A puree is a different dressing.
  • It is the cool side of a Buffalo plate, not the wing sauce.

Ingredients

  • 1/2 cup mayonnaise
  • 1/4 cup buttermilk
  • 1/2 cup crumbled blue cheese
  • 1 teaspoon lemon juice
  • 1/4 teaspoon black pepper
  • Pinch of salt

Steps

  1. Stir the mayonnaise, buttermilk, lemon, and pepper.
  2. Mash in half the cheese and fold in the rest.
  3. Chill 30 minutes.
  4. Taste before adding salt.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Yogurt can replace some mayonnaise.
  • A hotter dressing adds hot sauce and becomes a different dip.

Pairings

  • Buffalo wings
  • wedge salad
  • celery
