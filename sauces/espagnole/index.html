Espagnole

Brown stock, browned roux, and tomato: deep, roasted, and savory.

20 min prep · 1 hr cook · Serves 6 · about 2 cups

History

Espagnole is the brown mother sauce. The name is French for Spanish, a label that stuck in nineteenth-century French kitchens and does not mean the sauce is from Spain. Escoffier built it from brown stock, a brown roux, mirepoix, and tomato, then used it as the path to demi-glace.

Cultural background

It belongs to the professional French sauce pot: a base kept warm and reduced, not usually a table sauce by itself. Home cooks meet it mostly through demi-glace and pan sauces that borrow its flavor.

Why it tastes this way

The dark, roasted taste comes from a brown roux and browned vegetables, then brown stock. Tomato is a seasoning here, not the main flavor, which is why it does not taste like marinara.

How it is made

A brown roux thickens brown stock, with a browned mirepoix and tomato, then the sauce is skimmed and strained.

Authentic touch

Let the roux reach a true brown and a toasted smell before the stock goes in. A pale roux will not give you this sauce.

How it is used

Use it as the base of demi-glace or a pan sauce for steak. It is too plain and thick to be the whole point of a plate.

Field notes

  • The Spanish name is a French kitchen label, not a Spanish recipe.
  • A properly cooked brown roux thickens less than a white roux and tastes more toasted.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons butter or neutral oil
  • 1 small onion, diced
  • 1 small carrot, diced
  • 1 celery stalk, diced
  • 2 tablespoons all-purpose flour
  • 3 cups hot brown beef stock
  • 1 tablespoon tomato paste
  • 1 bay leaf
  • Salt

Steps

  1. Brown the onion, carrot, and celery in the fat.
  2. Stir in the flour and cook until the roux is brown and smells toasted, 4 to 6 minutes.
  3. Whisk in the stock and tomato paste. Add the bay leaf.
  4. Simmer 45 minutes, skimming the surface.
  5. Strain, season, and use as a base or reduce further.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Demi-glace reduces espagnole with more brown stock.
  • Sauce Robert adds mustard and onion to a brown sauce.

Pairings

  • pan sauces for steak
  • braised beef
  • demi-glace
