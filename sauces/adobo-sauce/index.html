Filipino Adobo Sauce

Vinegar, soy, garlic, and bay: sour, salty, and savory.

15 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 4 · about 2 cups with the chicken

History

Filipino adobo is a braise of vinegar, soy sauce, garlic, pepper, and bay. The Spanish word was applied to an older local vinegar cooking method. It is not Mexican adobo, which is a chile sauce. The sauce is the reduced braising liquid, served with the meat and rice.

Cultural background

Every region has a ratio. Some are more vinegary, some sweeter, some use coconut milk. This is the soy-and-vinegar chicken version.

Why it tastes this way

Vinegar and soy taste sour and salty. Garlic and bay perfume the reduced liquid. Filipino adobo is that vinegar braise, not Mexican chile adobo. The sauce should still taste sour after it reduces.

How it is made

Chicken simmers in vinegar, soy, and garlic until the liquid reduces to a sauce.

Authentic touch

Simmer until the liquid thickens and tastes sharp. If the sourness boils off, add a splash of vinegar back.

How it is used

Serve the chicken and the reduced sauce over rice.

Field notes

  • Filipino adobo and Mexican adobo share a name and not a recipe.
  • The vinegar should still be noticeable after the sauce reduces.

Ingredients

  • 1 pound chicken thighs
  • 1/2 cup vinegar
  • 1/4 cup soy sauce
  • 6 garlic cloves, smashed
  • 1 bay leaf
  • 1 teaspoon black pepper
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1/2 cup water

Steps

  1. Brown the chicken in the oil.
  2. Add the vinegar, soy, garlic, bay, pepper, and water.
  3. Simmer until the chicken is cooked and the sauce reduces, about 30 minutes.
  4. The sauce should taste sour and salty.
  5. Serve with rice.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A coconut-milk adobo is a Bicol-leaning variation.
  • Mexican chile adobo is a different sauce with the same Spanish word.

Pairings

  • rice
  • chicken
  • potatoes
