Detroit Coney Sauce

The next field stop is a nearby sauce on the same map.

Seasoned ground beef in a loose chili gravy: savory, spiced, and fine.

10 min prep · 40 min cook · Serves 8 · about 3 cups

History

Detroit coney sauce is the loose ground-beef chili on a Coney Island hot dog. Greek and Macedonian immigrants ran the lunch counters that made it a Detroit food in the early twentieth century. It is not a German sauce and it is not a Texas chili. No beans. The sauce is finely seasoned beef, spooned over a hot dog with mustard and onion.

Cultural background

A Coney is a specific Detroit hot dog, not every chili dog. The sauce should be fine and a little saucy so it sits in the bun. Heart is used in some older pots and is not required here.

Why it tastes this way

Beef, chili powder, and cumin taste like a mild chili. The grind is fine because the sauce has to cling to a small sausage. Mustard on the bun, not in the pot, is the sharp note. Greek and Macedonian lunch-counter cooking is the history. It is not a Scandinavian or German gravy.

How it is made

Ground beef is simmered with tomato, chili powder, and cumin until it is a fine sauce.

Authentic touch

Simmer the beef until it is fine and saucy, almost a paste. Spoon it on a steamed hot dog with yellow mustard and raw onion.

How it is used

Spoon it over hot dogs.

Field notes

  • Detroit’s coney sauce comes from Greek and Macedonian lunch counters, not from German immigrant cooking.
  • There are no beans in the sauce.

Ingredients

  • 1 pound ground beef
  • 1 small onion, minced
  • 2 tablespoons tomato paste
  • 1 tablespoon chili powder
  • 1 teaspoon cumin
  • 1 teaspoon paprika
  • 1 cup water
  • 1 teaspoon salt

Steps

  1. Brown the beef and onion, breaking the meat up very fine.
  2. Stir in the tomato paste and spices.
  3. Add the water and salt and simmer 30 minutes, stirring, until thick and fine.
  4. Add a splash of water if it dries.
  5. Spoon over hot dogs.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Some counters add beef heart. This pot uses ground beef only.
  • Cincinnati chili is a different Midwest sauce, also from Macedonian and Greek cooks, and it is served over spaghetti.

Pairings

  • hot dogs
  • yellow mustard
  • raw onion
