Ladolemono

Olive oil, lemon, and oregano: bright, salty, and clean.

5 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1/2 cup

History

Ladolemono is the Greek sauce of olive oil and lemon, often with oregano, used on grilled fish, salads, and vegetables. The name joins the words for oil and lemon. It is a table sauce and a basting sauce. It is not avgolemono, the egg-and-lemon sauce.

Cultural background

Grilled fish in Greece is unfinished without it. The ratio leans more oily than a sharp French vinaigrette, and dried oregano is the usual herb.

Why it tastes this way

Olive oil and lemon, with oregano, taste bright and clean. There is no egg, so it is a loose sauce for grilled fish, not avgolemono. The oil-forward ratio is the Greek grill taste.

How it is made

Lemon, oregano, and salt are whisked with olive oil.

Authentic touch

Whisk it again just before it hits hot fish. It separates as it sits, which is fine.

How it is used

Pour it over grilled fish, octopus, or a tomato salad.

Field notes

  • The name means oil-lemon, which is the whole method.
  • It is the grill sauce. Avgolemono is the soup and chicken sauce.

Ingredients

  • 1/4 cup olive oil
  • 2 tablespoons lemon juice
  • 1 teaspoon dried oregano
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • Black pepper

Steps

  1. Whisk the lemon, oregano, salt, and pepper.
  2. Whisk in the oil.
  3. Taste.
  4. Spoon over hot grilled fish.
  5. Whisk again if it separates.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A crushed garlic clove makes it sharper.
  • Avgolemono is the egg-thickened lemon sauce and a different recipe.

Pairings

  • grilled fish
  • octopus
  • Greek salad
