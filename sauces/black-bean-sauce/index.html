Black Bean Sauce

The next field stop is a nearby sauce on the same map.

Fermented black beans, garlic, and soy: salty, savory, and pungent.

10 min prep · 8 min cook · Serves 4 · about 1 cup

History

Cantonese black bean sauce is made with douchi, salted fermented black soybeans, rinsed, mashed with garlic, and stir-fried. It sauces clams, spare ribs, and beef. The beans are the savor. Rinsing them keeps the sauce salty rather than inedibly so.

Cultural background

A hot wok and a small amount of sauce are the method. The beans should be visible, not only a smooth gravy. Ginger and garlic are the aromatics.

Why it tastes this way

Fermented black beans taste salty and savory in a way fresh soy sauce does not. Garlic fried with them blooms that savor. A starch slurry makes a light glaze so the beans stay in bites.

How it is made

Rinsed fermented black beans and garlic fry, then soy and stock make a short sauce.

Authentic touch

Rinse the beans, mash them with garlic, and fry that paste before any liquid. Do not skip the rinse or the sauce will be too salty.

How it is used

Toss it with clams, beef, or greens.

Field notes

  • Douchi are fermented soybeans, not the black beans of Latin American cooking.
  • Rinsing is how you control the salt.

Ingredients

  • 2 tablespoons fermented black beans, rinsed
  • 3 garlic cloves, minced
  • 1 tablespoon ginger
  • 1 tablespoon oil
  • 1 tablespoon soy sauce
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 1 teaspoon cornstarch mixed with 1 tablespoon water

Steps

  1. Mash the rinsed beans with the garlic and ginger.
  2. Fry the paste in the oil 1 minute.
  3. Add the soy and water and simmer 2 minutes.
  4. Stir in the slurry until glossy.
  5. Toss with the food you are cooking.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Chile makes a hotter black-bean sauce.
  • A smooth jarred sauce is finer than this chunky home one.

Pairings

  • clams
  • beef
  • steamed greens
