Sakay

Fresh chile, garlic, and ginger: very hot and sharp.

15 min prep · 0 min cook · Serves 12 · about 3/4 cup

History

Sakay is the Malagasy chile condiment, a paste or oil of hot chiles with garlic and often ginger. It is on the table for rice, grilled meat, and stews. Heat levels vary, and the condiment is supposed to be hot. It is not a mild tomato ketchup.

Cultural background

A dab seasons a plate of rice. Families keep a jar. Ginger is a common partner to the chile and is included here.

Why it tastes this way

Fresh chile, garlic, and ginger pounded together taste very hot and sharp. It is a dab for rice, so it is concentrated. A mild ketchup would not season the plate the same way.

How it is made

Chiles, garlic, and ginger are pounded with salt and a little oil and vinegar.

Authentic touch

Pound it coarse, salt it, and use a small amount. It is a condiment, not a pouring gravy.

How it is used

Serve a small amount beside rice and grilled zebu or chicken.

Field notes

  • Sakay is a chile paste, used by the dab, not a pouring gravy.
  • Ginger is a frequent partner and is part of this version.

Ingredients

  • 8 fresh red chiles
  • 2 garlic cloves
  • 1 teaspoon grated ginger
  • 1/2 teaspoon salt
  • 1 tablespoon vinegar
  • 2 tablespoons oil

Steps

  1. Pound or blend the chiles, garlic, ginger, and salt.
  2. Stir in the vinegar and oil.
  3. Taste carefully.
  4. Refrigerate.
  5. Serve in small amounts with rice.

Refrigerate and use within two weeks. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • A red-only chile version without ginger is simpler.
  • Oil-covered paste keeps a little longer in the refrigerator.

Pairings

  • rice
  • grilled meat
  • beans
