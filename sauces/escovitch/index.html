Escovitch

Vinegar, onion, carrot, and scotch bonnet: sharp, sweet, and hot.

15 min prep · 10 min cook · Serves 6 · about 3 cups

History

Escovitch is the Jamaican pickled-pepper sauce for fried fish, related in name and method to Spanish escabeche. Hot vinegar is poured over onion, carrot, bell pepper, and scotch bonnet, then spooned on crisp fish. The pickle keeps and the vinegar cuts the fried fish.

Cultural background

Fried snapper and escovitch are a paired plate. The vegetables should stay slightly crisp. The chile perfumes the vinegar. Allspice is a frequent Jamaican note.

Why it tastes this way

Hot vinegar pickles the vegetables quickly, so they taste sharp and bright. Scotch bonnet is fruity and hot. A little sugar, if used, softens the vinegar. The fish is mild and fried, so the sauce has to be loud.

How it is made

Onion, carrot, and pepper sit under a hot vinegar boil with scotch bonnet and allspice.

Authentic touch

Boil the vinegar with allspice and chile, pour it over the sliced vegetables, and let it cool. Spoon it on fish that is still crisp.

How it is used

Spoon it over fried fish.

Field notes

  • The method is a quick pickle poured over fried fish.
  • Scotch bonnet and allspice are the Jamaican marks.

Ingredients

  • 1 onion, sliced
  • 1 carrot, julienned
  • 1 bell pepper, sliced
  • 1 scotch bonnet, sliced
  • 3/4 cup vinegar
  • 1/2 cup water
  • 1 teaspoon sugar
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 4 allspice berries

Steps

  1. Put the vegetables in a heatproof bowl.
  2. Boil the vinegar, water, sugar, salt, allspice, and chile 3 minutes.
  3. Pour the boil over the vegetables.
  4. Cool.
  5. Spoon over fried fish.

Refrigerate and use within a few days unless the recipe gives a different time. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Spanish escabeche is the older pickled-fish cousin.
  • More sugar makes a sweeter pickle.
