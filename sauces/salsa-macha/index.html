Salsa Macha

Dried chile and garlic fried in oil, with nuts: crunchy, smoky, and hot.

10 min prep · 12 min cook · Serves 10 · about 1 1/2 cups

History

Salsa macha is a Mexican oil-based chile sauce, especially associated with Veracruz, of dried chiles, garlic, and often peanuts or sesame fried until crisp. It is closer to a chile crisp than to a fresh pico. The oil is the sauce as much as the solids.

Cultural background

A spoonful seasons eggs, tacos, and fruit with chile and salt. Peanuts are common and are why this version is not nut-free. The chiles should fry until fragrant, not burnt.

Why it tastes this way

Dried chiles fried in oil taste toasted and smoky. Garlic and peanuts add savor and crunch. The oil carries the heat, which is why it stays potent in small amounts.

How it is made

Dried chiles, garlic, and peanuts fry in oil, then salt and vinegar are added.

Authentic touch

Fry the chiles and garlic in oil until they are crisp and fragrant, then cool them in that oil. Burnt chile tastes bitter.

How it is used

Spoon the oil and the crunchy bits over eggs, tacos, or vegetables.

Field notes

  • The oil is part of the sauce, not a byproduct.
  • It is a fried chile condiment, not a fresh tomato salsa.

Ingredients

  • 1 cup neutral oil
  • 6 dried árbol or guajillo chiles, stemmed
  • 6 garlic cloves, sliced
  • 1/4 cup peanuts
  • 1 teaspoon salt
  • 1 teaspoon vinegar

Steps

  1. Fry the peanuts and garlic in the oil until the garlic is golden. Remove them.
  2. Fry the chiles in the same oil until fragrant and slightly crisp.
  3. Cool, then stir the solids back in with salt and vinegar.
  4. Keep the oil.
  5. Spoon both oil and bits over food.

Refrigerate and use within a month. This is a home recipe, not professional food-safety advice.

Variations

  • Sesame instead of peanut changes the allergen and the taste.
  • A smoother blend is less typical.

Pairings

  • eggs
  • tacos
  • fruit with chile
