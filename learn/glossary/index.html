Glossary

Adobo
In Mexico, a chile sauce used as a marinade or stew base. In the Philippines, a braise of vinegar, soy, and garlic. The word is shared. The sauces are not the same.
Aïoli
A Provençal emulsion of garlic and olive oil. Many restaurant aïolis add egg and become a garlic mayonnaise.
Beurre manié
Soft butter worked with an equal weight of flour, then whisked into a sauce to thicken it at the end.
Bloom
To warm spices in fat so they smell fragrant before liquid is added.
Chutney
A South Asian relish, fresh or cooked, usually with acid, salt, and chili or spices. British and Caribbean kitchens adopted the cooked, sweet-sour style.
Coulis
A pourable puree, often of fruit or tomato, sometimes strained.
Deglaze
To loosen the browned bits in a pan with liquid.
Demi-glace
A brown sauce reduced with brown stock until it is glossy and thick enough to coat a spoon. A true demi-glace starts from espagnole.
Doubanjiang
Fermented broad-bean and chile paste, especially the aged paste of Pixian in Sichuan. It is the base of mapo tofu and many other Sichuan sauces.
Emulsion
A mixture of two liquids that do not normally stay together, such as oil and water, held by an emulsifier.
Fish sauce
A salty liquid from fermented fish, used across Southeast Asia. Nuoc mam and nam pla are examples. The dipping sauces made from it are separate recipes.
Gastrique
Caramel loosened with vinegar, used as the sharp-sweet base of sauces such as sauce bigarade.
Gochujang
Korean fermented chile paste made with gochugaru, grain, and fermented soy. Ssamjang and cho-gochujang are table sauces built from it.
Kecap manis
Indonesian sweet soy sauce, salty soy cooked or blended with palm sugar and spices.
Liaison
A finish of egg yolk and cream, tempered with hot liquid and added off the boil so it thickens without scrambling.
Mirepoix
A French base of onion, carrot, and celery cooked in fat. The Italian soffritto and the Cajun holy trinity are related but not identical.
Mole
A family of Mexican sauces, often including chiles, seeds or nuts, spices, and sometimes chocolate. Mole poblano is one mole, not the definition of all of them.
Monter au beurre
To whisk cold butter into a sauce off the heat so it turns glossy and emulsified.
Mother sauce
In the French repertoire, one of the five base sauces from which many named sauces are made.
Nam pla prik
Thai fish sauce with chile, often with lime and garlic. A table condiment, not a simmered sauce.
Nappé
Thick enough to coat the back of a spoon.
Nuoc cham
Vietnamese dipping sauce of fish sauce, lime or vinegar, sugar, water, garlic, and chile.
Palm sugar
Sugar from the sap of a palm, used in many Southeast Asian sauces. Brown sugar is a backup, not the same flavor.
Reduction
A liquid simmered until it concentrates.
Roux
Flour cooked in fat, used to thicken. The color ranges from white to brown.
Salsa
In Mexican and Central American cooking, a sauce that may be raw or cooked, usually with chile and tomatillo or tomato. The word simply means sauce in Spanish.
Sambal
A chile sauce of Indonesia and Malaysia. Sambal oelek, sambal terasi, and sambal matah are different recipes.
Shrimp paste
A pungent fermented paste, called belacan, terasi, kapi, or bagoong depending on the country. Cook it in the sauce unless the recipe is a raw relish that already accounts for it.
Slurry
Starch mixed with cold water, then stirred into simmering liquid to thicken it.
Sofrito
A seasoned base of aromatics. Spanish, Puerto Rican, Cuban, and Colombian sofritos do not share one recipe.
Tamari
A Japanese soy sauce brewed with little or no wheat. It is the usual gluten-free stand-in for soy sauce, and it tastes different.
Tare
A Japanese glaze or dipping sauce, often soy, mirin, sake, and sugar. Yakitori tare and unagi tare are two examples.
Temper
To warm a cold ingredient, such as egg yolk, with a little hot liquid so it does not curdle when it joins the pot.
Vinaigrette
A sauce of oil and vinegar, shaken or emulsified with mustard.
